Studs and duds from the Vikings' 19-15 loss to the Lions in Week 8

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

The Minnesota Vikings lost their first game of the 2026 season, losing 19-15 to the Detroit Lions at Ford Field on Sunday. After containing the Lions' running game in the first half, the Vikings struggled to slow down Jahmyr Gibbs as the game wore on.

But this loss still largely falls on the Vikings' offense, which amassed 266 total yards. Although they averaged 5.3 yards per play (up from 4.7 entering Week 8), they suffered five three-and-outs. This included their final possession of the game, which followed Detroit's seven-play, 69-yard touchdown drive to take the lead. The Lions then ran out the clock on an eight-play, 33-yard drive that began with 2:53 on the clock.

Minnesota is still 6-1, but the loss crowds the NFC North standings. Detroit is now 5-2, as are the Chicago Bears (who play the Seattle Seahawks on Monday), and the Green Bay Packers are 5-3. But the Vikings have another tough test next Monday night, hosting the 6-1 Buffalo Bills.

The season is far from over, but the same questions that surrounded Minnesota during their 11-game win streak dating back to last year came back to bite them on Sunday. Here are the studs and duds from the Vikings' loss to the Lions on Sunday.

Minnesota's pass rush sacked Jared Goff four times on Sunday. Andrew Van Ginkel led the charge with 2.5 sacks, although he had Goff in his grasp for another potential takedown. Overall, the Vikings blitzed Goff on 89.7% of his dropbacks, the most in the Next Gen Stats era (dating back to 2016).

The stat line doesn't tell the whole story on Kyler Murray. He threw two touchdowns and averaged 7.2 yards per pass. Murray also ran for 35 yards on four carries, including a 22-yard scamper in Minnesota's two-minute drill at the end of the first half. But he also threw a bad interception on the very next play and never appeared comfortable.

Murray still connected on a 51-yard pass to Jordan Addison in the fourth quarter and threw an 8-yard touchdown to T.J. Hockenson two plays later. But it's hard to make this list and not feel this offense needs to do more at quarterback.

Van Ginkel wasn't the only player to get after Goff in this one. Eric Wilson had a sack and two tackles for loss, helping bring the energy through most of the game. Last year, Wilson had two sacks in the Week 9 matchup against Detroit, so Brian Flores appears to like how Wilson's skill set can be utilized in these matchups.

The Jonathan Taylor trade rumors won't go away this week. Outside of Murray's attempts, the Vikings ran 14 times for 46 yards. Part of that was due to penalties that wiped out productive runs and set Minnesota up on 1st-and-long. Most of the time, the blocking wasn't good enough, and running backs couldn't make defenders miss to maximize opportunities. It's easy to blame Murray for this offense's struggles, but the run game hasn't been efficient enough this season.

His stat line wasn't spectacular (5 catches, 33 yards, 1 TD). But Justin Jefferson's spectacular 1-yard touchdown reception showed what happens when he gets an opportunity. Even when he appears covered, he can come down with the ball. This was the 17th time in Jefferson's last 18 games that he didn't eclipse 100 receiving yards. That may have played a role in him not speaking to reporters following the game.

I'm not going to absolve the defense of all blame. The Vikings took a 15-12 lead with 8:52 to play, but the Lions responded by running down their throat. Detroit ran five times for 57 yards on the drive, helping set up a play-action pass to Jameson Williams for a 7-yard touchdown. After containing Gibbs and the run game in the first half, Minnesota didn't appear to have many answers for Detroit's run game in the second half. The Lions finished the day rushing for 230 yards on 39 attemps.
