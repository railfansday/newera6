On Saturday, AC Milan will host Inter at San Siro for the first Derby della Madonnina of the season. It's a clash between two of the most important teams in Europe and the first derby for Portuguese coach Ruben Amorim, who joined AC Milan in the summer after leaving Manchester United in January. The two clubs will play at the iconic Italian arena, but on Thursday they unveiled plans for the new stadium that will replace San Siro, which both clubs currently share. The new San Siro will be named Generali Stadium after Italian insurer Generali acquired the naming rights in a deal worth €650 million ($725 million) over 20 years.

The project is a "71,500-seat stadium designed around one of football's most iconic atmospheres, setting a new standard for the live fan experience," according to the clubs. A bold architectural vision by Foster + Partners and MANICA, bringing together Lord Norman Foster and David Manica, the two architects who previously worked on the design of London's Wembley Stadium. The new arena will feature a striking X-frame structure and a sweeping double-curved roof, conceived to become Milan's next landmark. The stadium is a "defining investment" by RedBird and Oaktree, the owners of AC Milan and Inter, respectively, to "strengthen sporting competitiveness, unlock future growth and help shape the next era of Italian football." Two seating tiers form a continuous 360-degree bowl, creating exceptional sightlines and an immersive connection between fans and pitch. At either end, the Curvas rise as single, steep walls of support, drawn forward toward the field of play with seating inclinations of up to 37 degrees to amplify both atmosphere and visual impact. At the heart of the design is a distinctive structural form, whose powerful rhythm recalls both the legacy of Italian engineering and the intricate craftsmanship of the Duomo di Milano.
