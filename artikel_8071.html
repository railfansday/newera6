My 1-year-old cat Daisy has allergies. She is on a special hydrolyzed diet. Iâm trying to find an allergen-neutralizing spray to cut back on dust mites in the house. Would you recommend any specific products?

Since Daisy is already on a hydrolyzed diet, it makes sense to look at environmental allergens too, especially if her allergic signs are continuing. Dust mites are common household allergens, and while the goal is to reduce the amount of allergens in her environment, eliminating them completely is not realistic. Regularly washing bedding and blankets, vacuuming with a HEPA filter, and keeping dust to a minimum are still the best places to start.

If you would like to try a spray, Purefy Anti-Allergen Spray works differently from an insecticide. It is intended to neutralize or remove the allergenic proteins associated with dust mite debris, particularly the proteins found in their feces and other microscopic particles that accumulate in household dust and fabrics. Those proteins, rather than the living mite itself, are an important source of the allergic reaction. So the idea is not to kill every mite in the house, but to reduce the allergen they leave behind.

I would specifically avoid routinely using insecticide or acaricide sprays around Daisy, even when a product is marketed as ânatural.â Cats can be particularly sensitive to certain botanical ingredients and insecticides, including some essential oils and pyrethroids. If you use an anti-allergen spray, follow the label carefully, keep Daisy away from treated surfaces until they are completely dry, ventilate the area well, and never spray it directly on her or on surfaces she is likely to lick. Please ensure you also keep it away from food and water bowls.

Think of the spray as an extra layer of environmental control, not a treatment for Daisy's allergy. The unglamorous combination of washing, HEPA vacuuming, and reducing dust is still doing most of the heavy lifting. And if Daisy continues to have significant allergic signs despite a properly conducted hydrolyzed diet trial and environmental management, it is worth investigating other possible triggers rather than simply reaching for stronger household chemicals. Usually, the answer is not a more powerful spray, but figuring out exactly what is making your cat itchy. So, real detective work is often needed for success.
