One-Dish Red Curry Noodles

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Recipe by Susan Bronson, Rhinelander, Wisconsin

This one-dish red curry noodles recipe is packed with pantry and freezer staples, making it perfect as a dump-and-bake weeknight dinner.

I’m not quite sure where the time goes between school bus drop-off and dinnertime, but I’m pretty sure there’s a fast-forward button involved. Suffice it to say, quick and easy weeknight dinner recipes are life-savers. Easy casserole recipes made with pantry staples are some of my favorites, and dump-and-go recipes—which just require one dish—are even better. This one-dish red curry noodles recipe checks all those boxes: It’s a casserole; it takes just 45 minutes from start to finish; and it’s made with chewy udon noodles, colorful fresh and frozen vegetables, and a rich and spicy red coconut curry sauce.

Easy prep, minimal mess, quick to bake and something my whole family will eat? That’s a real win in my book.

Preheat the oven to 375°F. In a 13×9-inch casserole dish, stir together the vegetable stock, coconut milk, red curry paste, soy sauce, ginger, sesame oil and minced garlic.

Add the udon noodles and use a fork to carefully separate them into a single layer.

Top the noodles with the sliced red pepper, frozen stir-fry vegetables and finely shredded cabbage.

Submerge the noodles and vegetables in the liquid as much as possible.

Cover the dish tightly with foil and bake it for 20 minutes. Uncover the casserole dish and stir the noodles and vegetables. Return it to the oven, uncovered, and cook until the noodles are tender, about 15 to 20 minutes. Top the red curry noodles with green onions and sesame seeds, and serve them with lime wedges.

Editor’s Tip: When you pull the dish out of the oven the first time, take special care to separate any clumped noodles.

Any leftover red curry noodles should be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator.

When stored properly in the fridge, leftover one-dish red curry noodles last up to four days.

For the best flavor and texture, plan to serve this dish directly after baking it. However, you can prep the casserole up to 24 hours in advance, waiting to submerge the noodles in the sauce until just before it goes in the oven.

Unfortunately, this one-dish red curry noodles is not ideal for freezing. There’s a chance that the coconut milk will separate when thawed, leading to a grainy texture. Additionally, the softer texture of the cooked noodles will likely lead to a mushy texture once thawed.

This recipe calls for udon noodles, which are a thick type of Japanese noodle made with wheat flour. These thicker noodles hold up well in the hot oven, and retain their plump and chewy texture. You could also use egg noodles or lo mein noodles—even spaghetti will work in a pinch! Rice noodles are also an option, although the cook time will likely need to be shorter.

Pump up the protein in this noodle dish by adding cubed or shredded chicken, shrimp or ground pork. For plant-based protein, you can add cubed tofu, edamame, chickpeas or cannellini beans.

Whichever veggies you like best! A few of my family’s favorites include sliced bell peppers, broccoli, green beans and sugar snap peas. Frozen veggie stir-fry mixes, which commonly include broccoli, baby corn, water chestnuts and sugar snap peas, are a great short-cut too.
