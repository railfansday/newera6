Luminous viewpoint by Ryue Nishizawa rises within Japanese landfill site

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Photography by Setouchi Art and Industry

A giant orange cone designed by Japanese architect Ryue Nishizawa acts as an observation platform at this large recycling complex on Shikoku island in Japan.

Nishizawa, co-founder of Japanese studio SANAA, positioned the cone to overlook a large landfill basin at Oono Associates, a recycling complex in the city of Toon, and open up this closed-off industrial space to the public.

Named Observatory, it was created for the inaugural edition of Setouchi Art and Industry 2026, an art and architecture festival held across industrial sites in Japan.

Specifically, it is part of the Landscapes of Waste – A landscape shaped by nature, industry, and people exhibition, curated by architect Kazuyo Sejima, who co-founded SANAA with Nishizawa in 1995.

Nishizawa positioned the cone to overlook a large landfill basin within the vast recycling complex in Toon, Ehime, revealing the closed-off industrial setting to the public.

The bright orange cone measures 33 metres in diameter and four metres in height. Its size was intended to emulate an artificial hill.

Its luminous orange finish was chosen to deliberately contrast the blue-green hues of the surrounding landfill lining and landscape beyond it.

The platform encourages visitors to observe how the landscape is shaped by nature and human activity, and experience the processes of material circulation at the recycling plant.

Alongside Nishizawa’s observation platform, the Landscapes of Waste exhibition includes bus stops by architectural studio Shingo Masuda + Katsuhisa Otsubo, as well as a bus and visitor wear by architect Suzuko Yamada.

Visitors are required to take this bus to the site and put on the clothing, as seen in the photos. The festival team said this brings “visitors themselves into the composition of the landscape”.

“Together, these interventions invite a closer encounter with the relationship between nature, industrial infrastructure and human activity,” it added.

Setouchi Art and Industry 2026 was organised by the Japan Industrial Art Foundation. Artists and architects were invited to work with industrial sites across Okayama and the Ehime prefecture on Shikoku to create a “decentralised museum” that opens these settings to the public.

Its inaugural edition runs until 31 October across seven venues, which also include a salt production site, a textile factory, a shipbuilder and a battery manufacturer.

“Developed through collaboration between artists and participating companies, the exhibitions offer access to industrial spaces usually closed to the public, revealing the technologies, materials and people behind everyday life,” said Setouchi Art and Industry.

“We hope that many people, including the younger generation, will re-examine Japan’s industries from a fresh perspective and discover the charm and potential hidden within them,” added Japan Industrial Art Foundation director Masahiro Ito.

Other recent projects by SANAA include the Taichung Green Museumbrary in Taiwan and a proposal for the National Museum of Ecuador.
