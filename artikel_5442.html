PancakeSwap's 69% CAKE Emission Cut Would Make CAKE Less Deflationary

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

Cutting CAKE emissions by 69% sounds like the most token-friendly move PancakeSwap could make. But run last week's burn figures through "The Flip," and CAKE's net deflation doesn't speed up. It falls by about 30%.

That's the part missing from coverage of the vote, which is currently running about 98% against. Reports have focused on the drop from 22,500 to 7,000 CAKE per day. They haven't covered the burn the proposal removes to fund higher LP payouts. I rebuilt the supply math using PancakeSwap's own weekly burn breakdown. The result is more interesting than a straightforward "cut emissions, good for CAKE" story.

The Flip governance vote is a binary For/Against vote. It opened on October 21 at 08:07 UTC and closes on October 24 at 08:07 UTC. As of Thursday afternoon, the vote page showed:

One address, 0x3D...e1ad, cast 765,559 votes against, about 61% of all voting power used so far.

That share has fallen as more voters joined, and the result would be lopsided even without that wallet. Excluding it, "Against" still has more than 95%. With only 32 votes cast, though, this is mainly a verdict from a small group of large veCAKE holders, not a broad community poll.

The proposal, first posted on PancakeSwap's forum on October 14, has two parts.

Emissions: Daily CAKE emissions would drop from 22,500 to 7,000, with the remaining 7,000 going to a new ecosystem fund called the Pantry. The cut happens in two stages: first to 11,000 per day, then to 7,000 once CAKE reaches its Tokenomics 3.0 goal of about a 20% supply reduction.

PancakeSwap estimates LPs would have earned about $62 million more if this split had applied to 2025 volume.

Here's PancakeSwap's burn report for the week ending October 20, as published in its weekly update:

Current emissions are 22,500 per day, or 157,500 per week. That puts this week's net deflation at about 201,000 CAKE.

Now apply The Flip to the same week. The v3 and Infinity burns are mostly gone, while v2 and "Other" stay, leaving about 190,000 CAKE of weekly burn. At stage one, emissions fall to 77,000 a week, so net deflation drops to about 113,000 CAKE, a 44% decline. At stage two, emissions fall to 49,000 a week, and net deflation recovers only to about 141,000 CAKE. That's still 30% below where it is today.

The emission cut saves 108,500 CAKE a week at full rollout. But the removed v3 and Infinity burn totaled 168,600 CAKE that week. The burn being given up is larger than the emissions being saved.

The previous week tells the same story. Working backward from the week-over-week changes, the burn was about 530,000 CAKE: roughly 272,000 from v2 and 191,000 from v3. Net deflation was about 373,000. Under The Flip, it would have been about 249,000 at stage two, which is 33% lower.

There's one real offset. The non-stablecoin part of the new 12% treasury fee still funds buybacks, and PancakeSwap hasn't said how large that is. So my figures are a floor, not an exact forecast. It's also possible that higher LP payouts attract more liquidity and volume, and the team projects up to 30% more liquidity. That would increase v2 burns over time.

PancakeSwap's own estimate, based on 10 weeks of activity, is that v2 burns alone would keep CAKE net deflationary by about 190,000 CAKE a week after emissions. That's plausible, and CAKE would keep shrinking. But it's still a slower pace of deflation than what CAKE has been running at, sold under the headline of a 69% emission cut.

The final cut to 7,000 a day depends on reaching the 20% supply reduction target. CAKE supply is 329,915,408, down 11.5% from 372,837,045 in April 2025. The roughly 20% target sits near 298.3 million, which leaves about 31.6 million CAKE still to burn.

Coverage has suggested stage two could arrive about 12 months after stage one. Here's how long 31.6 million CAKE actually takes at stage-one net deflation:

Hitting the target in 12 months would require about 608,000 CAKE of net deflation every week. That's more than CAKE's gross burn in either recent week. On these numbers, stage one could stay in place for years. The "69% cut" headline is really a 51% cut for the foreseeable future.

LPs aren't the ones voting this proposal down. Voting power comes from veCAKE, which means locked CAKE, not liquidity positions. LPs are the group that stands to gain the most, with an 88% fee share.

veCAKE holders have different incentives. Today, they direct the 22,500 daily emissions through gauge voting, and an ecosystem has grown around that control. The Flip would reduce that budget to a 7,000-a-day fund managed by PancakeSwap's team. It would also cut the burn support that has been shrinking supply. For someone holding CAKE for its scarcity, The Flip gives up a definite burn today in exchange for a possible liquidity gain later.

That's why the vote is so one-sided. The people being asked to approve The Flip aren't the people it pays. If it fails on Saturday, I'd expect the next version to keep some v3 burn share. Otherwise, the emission cut doesn't do what the headline suggests.
