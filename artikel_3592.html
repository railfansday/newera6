The Mamdanis Take Manhattan!

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Billionaire Michael Bloomberg attended plenty of museum galas when he was mayor of New York City. Bill de Blasio didn’t—not even the Brooklyn Artists Ball at the Brooklyn Museum, a quick 30-minute walk up Prospect Park West from his beloved neighborhood of Park Slope. Eric Adams couldn’t wait to get his own tickets to art shindigs; in 2022, a few months into his one-and-only term as mayor, he arrived at the Met Gala red carpet wearing a long leather jacket emblazoned with the words “End Gun Violence.”

“I walk around with swagger in my pocket,” Adams told ABC News from the red carpet.

The city’s current head of government, Zohran Mamdani, is perhaps the most culturally literate mayor since Ed Koch. Or maybe ever—he instructed the city to invest over $320 million in the Department of Cultural Affairs, the largest amount in New York history. And he pays attention to the smaller sums too. When the department announced that city-funded grants for artists would double from $5,000 to $10,000, Mamdani filmed a video of himself, brush in hand, behind an easel, trying his best to paint dollar signs à la Warhol.

“Not everyone’s an artist!” he says, flashing that Mamdani grin. “But a lot of New Yorkers are.”

But when would he show up in a tux to one of the primo arts institution fundraisers that dot the social calendar, particularly as the fall season starts humming?

And now he has. Mamdani has officially attended his first art gala as mayor: the Studio Museum Gala, held at The Glasshouse this past Monday evening. Celebrating the museum’s first year in its new building, it raised over $4 million for the institution. While mingling among guests at the cocktail hour, Mamdani, who did indeed put on a tux for the occasion, posed for photos with First Lady Rama Duwaji and luminaries, such as collector Bernard Lumpkin and the artist Rashid Johnson.

The event was honoring longtime board chair Ray McGuire, the Lazard president and mega art collector who’s served as chair of the Studio Museum’s board for 23 years—and, in case you forgot, ran his own quixotic campaign for mayor in 2021. Through all the toasts, Mayor Mamdani and the first lady sat next to Studio Museum director Thelma Golden.

Mamdani has a bit of a history with the Studio Museum—it’s where he spent his most recent birthday, checking out work by Taylor Simmons, the Atlanta native currently in the museum’s residency program.

Rama Duwaji, Thelma Golden, Zohran Mamdani, Kathryn Chenault, Rashid Johnson, and Lake Bell

As it turns out, the world’s most famous mayor has had a hell of a week museum-going. On Monday, he also released a video filmed at The Met—it featured Mamdani standing in the Rockefeller Wing and wandering through the Great Hall. In it, he informs us that The Met is not just a place to check out pre-Columbian masterworks: It’s also a place to cast your vote.

“Only in New York City can you vote in places like the Metropolitan Museum of Art,” he says.

The Met and the Studio Museum are both part of the Cultural Institutions Group, a subset of New York cultural mainstays that receive funding from the city. Other institutions on that list include El Museo Del Barrio, the Brooklyn Museum and the Bronx Museum of the Arts. Cherished institutions such as, say, the Whitney Museum of American Art don’t receive city money, though that hasn’t stopped Mamdani from extending his support to them. A few weeks into his mayoralty, he alighted to the Whitney’s Renzo Piano–designed building in the Meatpacking District to announce a few mayoral appointments and a ban on junk fees on hotel bookings. He also gave the museum’s programming a shout-out.

“One of the last times I was here I saw the wonderful exhibition of Alvin Ailey,” he said, referring to “Edges of Ailey,” which was up at the Whitney in late 2024.

Later in the year, Duwaji stopped by one of the museum’s fundraisers, the Whitney Art Party—though the mayor did not attend with her. That evening, January 27, Mamdani instead made a surprise appearance on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon.

I’ve been tracking Mamdani’s interest in New York’s gallery scene since at least February 2025, when I was invited to a “Party for Zohran” at the Avenue A music venue Night Club 101. The event was co-hosted by Tribeca gallery Theta and Greene Naftali’s Carol Greene, as well as artists such as Martine Syms, Ser Serpas, and Aria Dean. “The art world is coalescing around a mayoral candidate,” the publicist Kaitlin Phillips, who was brought in to do PR for Mamdani early in his primary campaign, texted me in mid-February 2025, back when his campaign still seemed like a major long shot.

On March 2, I went to the party. After taking in some performances and live podcasts, Mamdani and I stepped outside to chat on Avenue A, talking about the art scene, which he was still getting fully acquainted with. He did, however, understand what it’s like to be an artistically minded person in New York.

“I think for me... Look, I’m the son of a filmmaker. I’m a failed artist myself,” he told me, though I still think the track kinda bangs. “I think that ultimately politics and art, there’s a commonality in the act of storytelling.”

I asked him about his platform for the arts in New York City.

“At the core is a belief that art is something that every New Yorker deserves in their life,” Mamdani said back then. “And that that is not something that can simply be restricted to the museums or a Museum Mile, but it is something that also is committed to the community organizations and neighborhoods in the outer boroughs that are, for many New Yorkers, the only conduit to having that.”

Since taking office, Mamdani has named Diya Vij, an arts-organizations veteran, to lead the Department of Cultural Affairs, and assembled a slew of heavyweights to serve on his Committee on Arts and Culture. He’s also started picking board members for various museums. His campaign deputy Elana Leopold sits on the MoMA board, while Battery Park City CEO Raju Mann sits on the board of MoMA PS1.

As far as we know, though, no word has surfaced on who will be the mayor’s rep on The Met board—the most powerful perch of them all. Adams picked Scott Sartiano as his rep early in his term, and his fellow board members have indicated to me that Sartiano was an active board member who was pivotal and energizing for the team. (I wrote a whole profile on Sartiano, pegged to the rise of Zero Bond, back in 2024.)

But I’m sure it’ll be a thoughtful pick. Even through his meteoric rise, the mayor’s platform for artists in New York hasn’t really changed since that night we chatted on Avenue A.

“Artists have brought imagination and honesty to every corner of our city—from our sidewalks and subways to our museums, parks, and theaters. For generations, they have come to New York to explore, create, and challenge convention, shaping our city’s identity and drawing people from around the world,” Mayor Mamdani told me in a statement. “My administration is committed to supporting them and ensuring every New Yorker can experience the arts. We doubled city-funded grants for artists for the first time in nearly 20 years, and we’re partnering with organizations like TDF, The Met, and cultural institutions across the city to expand access to free and low-cost Broadway and opera tickets, as well as free admission to more than 20 cultural institutions. Because the arts are essential to who we are as a city, and every New Yorker should have the opportunity to experience them.”
