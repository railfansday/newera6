How to Start a Workout When You Really Don’t Feel Like It (The Bridge Task Method)

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

If you can’t get yourself to start a workout after a long day, the problem might not be the workout itself. It’s the gear shift from “couch” to “go.”

A bridge task is a small, neutral action (not fun, not hard, just…meh) that you put in between the two so the jump is smaller.

Below is the 5-step version one of our coaching clients uses with her own students, plus a free worksheet to map your own.

A few weeks ago I was talking with Tabitha, a Nerd Fitness coaching client, about a challenge a lot of people face. ​ Some quick context: Tabitha is an executive function coach. She helps neurodivergent students and their families build the skills and systems that support their academic and personal goals. ​ And she noticed something: ​ The challenge of getting started on a workout is almost identical to the challenge her students face when trying to start homework. ​ This might sound familiar. You get home after a long day, mentally fried, and all you want to do is veg out on the couch. ​ In that state, getting up to cook a healthy dinner or go do a workout can feel really freaking hard.

Switching from a “YEAH!” task (chilling on the couch) to a “NAH!” task (workout/homework), is often harder than the task itself.

If we can make that gear shift less dramatic, it can make a big difference!

Instead of going from YEAH! to NAH!, you insert at least one task that’s just…MEH.

If you’re struggling to start your workout, don’t fight the gear change. Bridge it.

Add a little MEH to the mix and see what happens.

Tabitha put together a free worksheet that walks you through mapping your own task initiation sequence. Your personal YEAH -> MEH -> NAH.

Try it this week, and keep me posted on how it goes. ​ You got this. ​ -Matt
