Clemson survives early scare vs. Radford, secures win in season opener

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Nov. 2—CLEMSON — It was two days late for Halloween, but Clemson women's basketball gave itself an early scare in its season opener against Radford.

The Tigers took down their Big South foe 68-47 on Nov. 2 and start the year 1-0. The two teams went into the locker room tied 27-27 at halftime before a 3-point surge and strong defensive effort in the second half lifted Clemson past what would've been a hefty season-opening loss. Radford — which is no scratch as a mid-major, being picked as its conference's preseason co-favorite — will be paid $26,500 for the game, according to the game contract obtained by The Post and Courier via public records request.

"We should be bigger and faster, and maybe a little stronger. But they're going to give us a test," Clemson coach Shawn Poppie said. "They did."

Clemson came out to an early 9-0 lead and made its first basket on a 3-pointer from Virginia Tech transfer guard Mackenzie Nelson. She led the team in scoring with 16 points. Poppie said in preseason he wants Nelson, who averaged 8.0 points and 5.7 assists per game with the Hokies last season, to score more than she ever has this year.

"I think that I'm still the same basketball player. I just kind of shifted my mindset a little bit," she said. "Not always pass first. Take what they give you, and if that's the pass, if that's the shot, take it."

The Tigers quickly ran cold, making just one more three on 12 total attempts in the first half. Radford pulled in close and traded baskets with Clemson thanks to some solid paint play: The Highlanders outscored Clemson 20-18 on the interior and tied the Tigers with 18 rebounds.

Things settled in like one would expect from a buy game in the second half. The Tigers made three straight 3-pointers early after the break to pull ahead and continued to be steady shooters from there, finishing 43.3 percent from the floor and 29.6 percent from three.

Clemson held the Highlanders scoreless for the last nine minutes of the 10-minute third quarter.

From there it was all Tigers as they rolled to the finish in the fourth quarter and forced five Radford turnovers as the Highlanders' early momentum couldn't be carried out of the break.

"Some of it's just focus, right? I mean, I love (Nelson), but she throws a junior-high overhead pass into the post because you're just not focused, and I think sometimes you just take it for granted a little bit," Poppie said. "You just got to lock in and make the simple plays."

Clemson turned the ball over eight times in the first half and zero times in the second half.

Three Clemson players — Mackenzie Nelson (16), Taliyah Henderson (15) and Holland Harris (11) — scored in double-digit figures.

Twelve of Clemson's 13 available players were on the floor in the game, including Clemson's freshman trio of Kimora Fields, Julia Scott and Meeyah Green. All three scored their first career field goals in the game. Poppie said he'll be challenged to figure out the formula to maximizing Clemson's depth.

Mia Moore, who was granted a sixth season of eligibility by the NCAA in October, finished with nine points, three rebounds and two assists.

Clemson guard Rusne Augustinaite, who tied the program's single-season program record with 80 made threes last season, went 0 for 7 from behind the line on Monday.

Heading into the half, it was clear Clemson needed to improve its shooting from the outside to pull ahead. Head coach Shawn Poppie's teams shoot threes at a high volume, which can backfire in situations like a 2 for 12 first half.

"We'll make way more than we made. ... We just talk about getting good shots," Poppie said. "I thought that we got in a rhythm in the second half. That's what we were doing, and then the ball just magically starts to go in. So, no, we don't put a number on it. We got to get good shots."

It was clear early in the second half that shooting woes wouldn't plague the Tigers all game. Clemson made back-to-back-to-back threes, plus a transition layup from Henderson to pull ahead, and never relinquished the lead again.

Clemson hosts ETSU on Nov. 5 at 7 p.m. The game will be aired on ACCNX.

"When you're looking at a schedule, I knew this was maybe not the smartest of things to do, but when you're going to play a mid-major, let's go play one that we think is going to win a lot of games," Poppie said. "Come February, March, we're talking about seeding, and that's our plan, a game like this helps. Because you're going to look back and they're going to win 20-something games."
