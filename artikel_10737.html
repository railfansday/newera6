British man who spent 28 years walking around the world finally makes it home

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

A British adventurer is on the verge of completing a 28-year journey around the world, having travelled roughly 31,000 miles to reach his hometown.

Karl Bushby, a 57-year-old former paratrooper from Hull, set off from Chile on November 1, 1998, determined to make his way home without using transport to advance his journey.

What was originally intended to take 12 years turned into almost three decades of walking and swimming that saw him overcome everything from dangerous wildlife to international border disputes.

Now, on November 2, 2026, Bushby is due to complete his expedition by arriving at his mother’s house in Hull, 28 years and one day after setting out.

Known as the Goliath Expedition, Bushby’s journey took him across four continents and around 30 countries, encountering extreme temperatures, dangerous terrain, and numerous setbacks.

Speaking to The Guardian, Bushby explained that he had dreamed of going on a major adventure since childhood. His eventual plan was inspired by a birthday card from his father, which described SAS soldiers discussing the possibility of walking from London to New York.

Among his biggest challenges was crossing the frozen Bering Strait between Alaska and Russia in 2006, where he faced freezing conditions and even encountered a polar bear.

He was subsequently detained in Russia, while visa restrictions, financial problems, and the COVID-19 pandemic caused further delays.

“You can’t account for financial crises, pandemics, visa bans, wars and the bureaucracy you have to deal with,” he told The Guardian, explaining why it took so long to complete.

More recently, Bushby spent 31 days crossing the Caspian Sea before swimming the English Channel, finally reaching British soil on October 5.

But the journey also came at a significant personal cost. According to Associated Press, Bushby hasn’t seen his mother in around 10 years, while his son largely grew up without him. He also has a 12-year-old granddaughter he’s never met.

Despite waiting decades for this moment, the prospect of returning to normal life has also left him apprehensive.

“My purpose for living is about to abruptly end,” he told The Guardian, explaining that he would need to find a new direction after spending so much of his life travelling.

According to Hull City Council, Bushby is expected to receive a public welcome on November 2, before continuing to his family home to officially complete the journey.
