Trump loses supreme court bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots as justices refuse to take up emergency request – live

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Ruling was latest judicial rebuke of unprecedented efforts from Trump administration to intervene in elections process of multiple states

Supreme court rules against Trump over bid to force California to set aside ballots

The US supreme court on Monday quickly rejected the Trump administration’s request to force California to set aside thousands of mail ballots from people President Donald Trump’s administration claims are noncitizens, the Associated Press reports.

The high court refused to intervene in a case involving 20,000 people the Republican administration flagged as likely noncitizens on the largely Democratic state’s voter rolls. The decision came just hours after the administration asked the supreme court to intervene.

The ruling was the latest judicial rebuke in the face of a wave of unprecedented efforts from the Trump administration to intervene in the elections process of multiple states. The midterm elections are expected to bring nationwide victories for Democrats and impose more Congressional checks on Trump’s power.

In California, the Trump administration had asked the high court to set aside the challenged ballots until the voters’ citizenship status could be verified. But officials there said that the list appeared to be filled with errors and that setting the ballots aside could effectively disenfranchise legal voters.

The high court did not detail its reasoning for rejecting the Trump administration’s request, as is common in emergency decisions. The justices previously rejected Trump’s push to restrict mail ballots around the country in September, even though the justices have sided with his administration in a series of other emergency docket appeals.

US negotiators have been seeking a breakthrough in the long-running conflict before the winter sets in. The last US-mediated peace talks took place ⁠in February before the United States and Israel launched a war against Iran.

It’s worth noting just what a bureaucratic nightmare it would have been if the supreme court had sided with the Trump administration’s request that the court order California to identify and set aside 20,000 ballots that it claimed were potentially submitted by noncitizens.

As the Associated Press notes, voting has been underway in California, a largely Democratic state, since early October. All of the state’s roughly 23 million registered voters are mailed a ballot. Hundreds of thousands of ballots have already been processed. California election officials have said that finding and setting aside the challenged ballots now would be logistically impossible.

California officials are celebrating the Supreme Court ruling. Governor Gavin Newsom called the Trump administration’s attempts to intervene in the state’s election a “baseless attack.”

“After repeatedly losing in the lower courts, the highest court in the land firmly slapped down the Trump DOJ’s baseless attack on California’s electoral process — not even entertaining it,” Newsom said in a statement. “The third branch of government did its job, and that should be a welcome sight for all Americans given the disgusting and dangerous attacks on our democracy by the federal government. Tomorrow, Californians can feel secure in knowing the state will carry out a free and fair election — just as we’ve always done.”

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said state officials “will remain vigilant and keep fighting back against federal efforts to undermine our democracy.”

“Courts have now rejected the Trump Administration’s attempts to interfere in California’s elections not once, not twice, not three times, but four times — from the district court to the Ninth Circuit to the U.S. Supreme Court,” said Bonta in a statement. “This latest decision should put an end to the Administration’s attempt to disrupt California’s elections through emergency court orders.”

California Secretary of State Shirley N. Weber said that Californians can now focus on voting.

“While this fight is not over yet, on the eve of Election Day, this means millions of Californians can focus on the most important task at hand — casting their ballot,” Weber said.

The Center for Election Innovation & Research (CEIR), a non-profit that focuses on restoring trust in the election system, celebrated the Supreme Court’s decision to reject the Trump administration’s request to intervene in the California election.

CEIR Executive Director David Becker said the Supreme Court reaffirmed “state sovereignty by swiftly rejecting the Justice Department’s illegal attempt to interfere with California’s election.”

The Trump administration had asked the Supreme Court to set aside thousands of mail-in ballots from California residents the administration claimed were non-citizens. State officials had previously said the list was full of errors.

Two losses in two courts in just three hours.

Judges across the country have repeatedly rebuked the Trump administration for its last-minute demand that California set aside 20,000 ballots that the administration claims it has evidence were cast by noncitizens —an unprecedented and possibly unconstitutional attempt to interfere with a state’s electoral process.

Today, the administration’s California gambit was rejected by two different courts in two separate cases in a matter of hours.

First, a federal judge in Washington ordered the Trump administration to stop demanding that California election officials set aside ballots from 20,000 people, writing, “The Executive Branch has likely violated the Constitution’s Elections Clause, which commits authority over election rules to the states and Congress alone.”

Then, in a separate case, the supreme court swiftly rejected the Trump administration’s emergency request this morning that it intervene on the day before the election and force California election officials to set aside the 20,000 mail ballots.

Democratic elections attorney Marc Elias called the supreme court ruling “A complete DEFEAT for Trump DOJ” noting that the court’s denial of the administration’s request came with “no noted dissents,” even from more conservative justices, and that the court “denied DOJ’s application about the California election without even asking for us to respond.”
