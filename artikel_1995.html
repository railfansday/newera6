'They'll be back': Scott Bain issues honest Celtic verdict after Falkirk heroics

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Scott Bain knows a thing or two about Celtic crises. The goalkeeper spent almost a decade at Parkhead, with the infamous 2020/21 campaign a rare blemish on an otherwise admirable CV.

Bain won seven league titles, five Scottish Cups, and four League Cups with Celtic. He predominantly served as a back-up stopper throughout his spell at the club, registering 78 appearances in seven-and-a-half years.

He came back to haunt his ex-employers on Sunday, making nine saves as Falkirk recorded their first win over Celtic since 2007.

Martin O'Neill's side have now suffered six defeats in their last 10 matches, looking anything but a structured, effective unit.

Bain, though, insists he's seen Celtic in worse states, and that it won't 'take much' for them to once more become the 'unbeatable' team he played in.

"I feel sorry for boys in terms of it's a difficult place to be when it's not going well. But I've seen them worse," Bain said. "They've been worse and I was there when they were worse.

"So I think there's obviously overreaction at times with fans and things like that because it's a very emotional club and it's a club that's rooted deep in people's families and across generations. So they demand a lot of you.

"The only way to get through it is to stand up and puff your chest out and keep going, you can't buckle to the fans or the pressure or the results. They've been fine in the league.

"Obviously the last two results are a bit damning for them but in terms of the league I don't feel there's been any issues and I think maybe these little issues here have probably been brought on from outside noise and extra pressures on to the players.

"Obviously they've not performed as well in Europe as Celtic have recently, outwith your Champions League disasters, if I can say that."

Scott Bain greets Liam Scales after Falkirk's win over Celtic. (Image: Mark Runnacles / Shutterstock)

During what's now known as the Covid season, Bain played 23 times. It remains Celtic's only trophy-less campaign since 2009/10.

"I never felt the full brunt of it but the Covid season was really tough and that wasn't even fans in the stadium," Bain explained. "I think no fans made it even worse.

"But for the majority of my time there it's been sweet, the club was in a really good place and I think it'll maybe take a few signings here and there, a few ideas here and there and they'll be back there, I don't think it'll take much."

Bain left Celtic for Falkirk last summer. He's since become one of their most important players, earning a recall to the Scotland national team.

Inconsistency of minutes at Celtic, Bain says, made finding his peak form 'impossible'.

"When I played for Celtic we were really unbeatable when I played the majority of my games so I didn't really feel that until I came in with the signing of [Vasilis] Barkas and I played a game here and there, and I always found it difficult in general to play a game, come out of the team and play a game a few months later, play a couple of games here and come out.

"And then eventually I played a game once a year - what are you supposed to do with that? It's almost impossible."

"Could be, aye," was Bain's modest response when asked if Sunday was the best performance of his career.

Did any of his old Celtic chums, then, say anything post-match?

"They did, aye, a few expletives! No, they're obviously disappointed," Bain revealed. "It's a club that demands a lot of you.

"They created a lot of chances in the game and probably would win any sort of other game in that sense, it was just one of those days.

"But, look, I'm really tight with a lot of those boys and I've spent a lot of my life really with some of them. So they're disappointed but the boys that I know have congratulated me in terms of what a performance it was."
