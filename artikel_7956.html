Back in 2010, the founders of Fantastic Frank sized up standard real estate broker fare and vowed to do things better. Sharing a love of architecture and design, the four friends committed to being selective not only about the properties they sell but how they’re presented: from the start, teams of stylists and fashion photographers have been deployed to capture each listing at its most seductive, making Fantastic Frank’s offerings the opposite of blank and boring—and a great source of interior inspiration.

The approach paid off: the Stockholm-based firm has since gone global: it now has offices in, among other places, Berlin, Lisbon, Mexico City, and NYC. Masters at showing other people’s quarters, the company recently had to figure out a fresh guise for itself when it prepared to move into new headquarters in Stockholm. ASKA, an emerging Swedish firm led by architect Polina Sandström, was hired to transform a hodgepodge of spaces in a historic building into Fantastic Frank’s showcase. Wanting to preserve existing, characterful elements while unifying the office, Sandström made masterful use of the firm’s old office furniture, a tightly orchestrated materials palette, and, most notably, a beguiling shade of pistachio. “Although we don’t specify the paint brand,” continues Sandström, “we always specify the sheen, since it contributes significantly to how the color and surface are perceived. Here, we chose a very matte wall finish with a sheen level of 5, and a matte finish for the joinery with a sheen level of 20, giving the space a more contemporary expression.”
