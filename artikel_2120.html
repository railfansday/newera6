Kretinsky faces challenge to show he’s no David Sullivan 2.0 | West Ham News

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

West Ham’s last three managers have all faced a gradual, game-by-game countdown of deadlines, drama and demands for improvement, which all failed and eventually led to their demise. Who can forget the painful series of ‘must win’ games that both Lopetegui and Graham Potter endured – seemingly without an end – as David Sullivan kicked the can down the road rather than making clear cut, cold, calculating decision to cut the ties with a failing manager.

Sullivan in fact became something of a laughing stock after proclaiming ‘this one is a must – win‘ and then famously  sitting on his hands and not sacking Potter after he’d duly failed to win.

Of course the irony was that all three were dispatched eventually – in differing circumstances but dispatched nonetheless.

How many points would have been saved if Sullivan had fired Potter earlier on in the season?

Worst ever, yet stumbled on with endless ‘must win games’ to try and improve results: Graham Potter was fired eventually anyway and quite possibly the delay cost West Ham their Premier League status

Not just my opinion, but pretty much the favour of the lunchtime West Ham YouTube channels too – that Kretinsky needs to be bold and decisive and prove he’s not simply following the David Sullivan modus operandi and merely kicking the Management can down the road.

A bad manager doesn’t suddenly become one worth saving  merely because of a possible lucky win away at Cardiff on Wednesday night, as Russ from the west ham network points out today in his video entitled “Why are West Ham waiting to sack Nuno| That’s not leadership, that’s indecision”

In essence, Kretinsky – who shies away from the limelight, but according to our own Gonzo on hammers chat, has something of a record of being ruthless with his firing of managers over at Sparta Prague. The Sphinx needs to bring that ruthlessness to bear at West Ham and prove he’s not merely a carbon copy of the former chairman – a David Sullivan 2.0.

Kicking the managerial can down the road merely to save a few million in compensation is a false economy, with promotion to the Premier League reportedly being worth £200 million. Automatic promotion, the club’s stated aim, is looking a very unlikely possibility under Nuno, with West Ham on a downward dive and currently in eighth position.
