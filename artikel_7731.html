Court finds law protecting judges, officers’ personal information unconstitutional

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

U.S. District Judge Esther Salas of the District of New Jersey delivers remarks at a news conference in Newark, New Jersey, on Dec. 19, 2022. (Photo By Kyle Mazza/Sipa USA/Sipa via AP Images)

A West Virginia law shielding the online publication of personal information belonging to judges and law enforcement officers was ruled unconstitutional by a federal appeals court Tuesday.

The Oct. 27 ruling by the 4th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals at Richmond, Virginia, is the first instance of a federal appellate court overriding a state law protecting the home addresses and unpublished phone numbers of judges and officers since states began safeguarding personal information after a gunman shot and killed 20-year-old Daniel Anderl, the son of U.S. District Judge Esther Salas of the District of New Jersey, inside their New Jersey home in 2020.

West Virginia passed its law in 2021. In 2022, Congress passed the Daniel Anderl Judicial Security and Privacy Act to ⁠protect federal judges’ personal information online.

Class action lawsuits filed by Michael Jackson, a retired law enforcement officer, charged Thomson Reuters, Whitepages Inc., PeopleConnect and LexisNexis with violating the state’s Daniel’s Law through online people search services, according to coverage by Reuters.

A Thomson Reuters spokesperson said it “has long been committed to honoring privacy and keeping personal data safe. … But in its current form, the statute is overbroad and violates the First Amendment.”

Bursor & Fisher attorney Philip Fraietta, who represents Jackson, did not give a comment for the article.
