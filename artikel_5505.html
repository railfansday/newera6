Intel stock's record October plunge puts its foundry ambitions to the test

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Intel stock fell 29% on Friday after the company issued a fourth-quarter revenue forecast of $15 billion to $16 billion

Intel $INTC is under mounting pressure to make its foundry business work after its stock lost 45% of its value in October, the largest one-month decline ever recorded, according to CNBC.

Intel stock shed 29% on Friday, a one-day loss it had not seen since the 1970s, according to CNBC. The drop followed the company's Thursday release, in which it reported third-quarter results and forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion and non-GAAP earnings of 25 cents a share. The stock slid a further 8% during Monday's session. The revenue forecast fell $1 billion to $2 billion short of analyst estimates, according to CNBC.

Third-quarter revenue rose 21% to $16.5 billion. Data center and AI revenue rose 78% to $7.3 billion, while client computing revenue fell 5% to $8.1 billion. CFO David Zinsner said the company expects the PC downturn to persist for at least the first six months of next year.

Intel Foundry revenue rose 58% to $6.7 billion. The unit posted an operating loss of $1.85 billion, compared with a loss of $2.32 billion a year earlier. Intel said it released the Intel 14A 0.9 process design kit in October.

The foundry business has yet to sign a flagship client for Intel's leading-edge chips, and most of what it produces goes into Intel's own products, according to CNBC. The company has put roughly five years of effort, and much of the $108 billion it invested in capital projects over that time, into taking on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing.

Intel said it is redirecting part of its Oregon factory output away from 18A wafers and toward wafers built on the older Intel 3 process that go into its data center products, according to CNBC. Intel is banking on 18A to win over external customers, which means the reallocation could make that push harder.

David Yoffie, a Harvard Business School professor who sat on Intel's board for nearly 30 years, said prospective customers worry about capacity, according to CNBC. "They're concerned that if Intel doesn't actually have excess capacity beyond its own demand, that they're just not a very credible supplier," Yoffie said. He said the pressure on Intel's finances will intensify if it fails to sign a major foundry client in the next 12 months, and that spinning off the foundry business is "maybe the only solution."

CEO Lip-Bu Tan said on the earnings call that "a couple of customers" would be ready to announce over the next two quarters, according to CNBC. Tan has held back on spending until customers have firmly committed to buying.

Intel in July announced a collaboration with Fortinet $FTNT to make Fortinet's next-generation SP6 security processor, the first publicly named cybersecurity chip customer for its foundry business. The chip will be built on Intel 4, a more mature process node than 14A and 18A.

An April regulatory filing showed Intel had yet to land a significant customer for its most advanced manufacturing processes.
