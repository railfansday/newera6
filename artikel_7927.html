Wild Bleeding Heart: A Hardworking and Adaptable Beauty

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

When you garden with ecology in mind, choosing plants becomes less about you and more about the local birds, mammals, and invertebrates that could benefit from your latest addition. Here, Melissa Starkey shares about wild bleeding heart, one of her favorite underappreciated native plants to grow.

Watch: Super Cool Plants Podcast | Underappreciated Native Plants

This plant is extremely close to my heart because I remember seeing it growing wild in Pennsylvania when I was a child. A great alternative to nonnative common bleeding heart (Lamprocapnos spectabilis and cvs., Zones 3–9), it produces its best flush of heart-shaped flowers in spring but keeps blooming through the entire growing season, even as late as November here at Mt. Cuba Center. Wild bleeding heart also has fernlike foliage, which has a kind of blush tinge.

But all its aesthetic qualities aside, wild bleeding heart is incredibly hardworking and adaptable. It can grow in partial shade and makes an excellent woodland plant. This plant also does quite well out in the sun; it grows naturally on rocky, sunny outcrops. I have seen it growing right out of rocks in the wild. While it does adapt well, its unique flowers require larger pollinators, like bumblebees, that are strong enough to force themselves through its tight petals.

Wild bleeding heart is a bit smaller than common bleeding heart, but it does not go dormant like its nonnative relatives, giving you three seasons of interest. If pink flowers aren’t to your taste, there is a white cultivar available called ‘Alba’ (D. eximia ‘Alba’, Zones 3–9).

Learn more from Melissa about this plant on Fine Gardening’s podcast, Super Cool Plants, or watch the episode on our YouTube channel!

Melissa Starkey is the marketing and communications manager and an instructor at Mt. Cuba Center, a botanical garden in Hockessin, Delaware.
