Everton give Burnham something to shout about by booking quarter-final spot

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

David Moyes took a glug of water and glanced up at the big screen away to his right, almost as if he wanted confirmation that what he was seeing was true.

Had he looked behind him, Moyes would have seen Andy Burnham and his wife, Marie-France, in raptures, along with thousands of others. It was true, all right. Everton had scored their third goal and were bounding into the Carabao Cup quarter-finals, seemingly without a care in the world.

What a happy change of direction. Moyes has spent months spinning plates, dealing with owners who do not care about a proud club and want to sell up and a squad that was left criminally short in the transfer window. Then, before Newcastle’s visit, came more disruption.

Jack Grealish’s selfish and reckless decision to go Awol from training on Monday could have been a catalyst for more misery and invited Newcastle to deliver a hammer blow to Everton’s season, but again, Moyes fostered a siege mentality. He was rewarded in the most emphatic fashion.

True, Newcastle collapsed like a deckchair without hinges and this performance – which encouraged many of their 8,000 followers to head for the exits early – would not have done anything to convince those who view Matthias Jaissle, the head coach, with ambivalence to change their minds.

The lack of discipline in their ranks was exposed when Dan Burn scored a late consolation and ended up in a melee with James Tarkowski and Joelinton. But the issues Jaissle, who lamented that “we must improve”, has to correct are for another day: this contest belonged to Everton.

Thierno Barry had provided the finish that secured a trip to Fleetwood in December and what an opportunity it is for Moyes to make Everton semi-finalists in this competition for the first time since 2016. Whether Grealish will have a part to play in it remains to be seen.

The last thing Everton needed on a night of such importance was a distraction. Grealish will have a tough job mending relations, particularly with Moyes, but there was a paradox: though he had caused a major issue for 90 minutes he became an irrelevance.

Moyes is impatient to give Evertonians a big day out and his only focus was on crossing this hurdle – for all that Newcastle have had a turbulent start to the campaign, they are dangerous opponents and you wondered in the fraught opening 30 minutes if they might exacerbate the hosts’ woes.

As Jaissle hopped and squirmed on the sidelines, imploring Newcastle forward, chances came and went: Harvey Barnes had a header saved, Fabian Schar tested Jordan Pickford from 30 yards while Vitalii Mykolenko and James Tarkowski made huge goal-saving challenges.

“We should have scored more goals,” Jaissle, who was dressed as if auditioning for the role of The Milk Tray Man, growled. “It [losing] was shocking as the game was not that bad.”

Moyes was infuriated by Everton’s initial inertia, so much so there was a point when the Scot prevented Merlin Röhl, his own full-back, from taking a throw-in until Tarkowski and Jake O’Brien had complied with his gesturing to get 10 yards further forward.

It might have looked old-fashioned but it worked. Suddenly Everton started asking questions, Hayden Hackney almost got on the end of a cross from Mykolenko, and it encouraged the supporters on the vertiginous South Stand to raise their voices and become more involved.

What they needed was a goal to set things alight and in the 34th minute, it arrived. Tyrique George won possession by Newcastle’s corner flag, the ball dropped at Hackney’s feet and he instinctively ushered it on to James Garner, who whipped a right-footed drive beyond Lukáš Horníček.

The roar that greeted it was a mix of elation and wonder, relief and desperation. The locals have grown weary of seeing nights with potential end in disappointment but here was the foothold they craved and the opportunity they were ready to seize. Soon the lead would be doubled.

Hackney would score the goal, converting a super Barry cross, but let us acknowledge the fabulous refereeing of Farai Hallam, who waved play on when he might have whistled for a foul on Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall. And how Everton took advantage.

It must be said the officiating this week has, in the main, been outstanding. Watching a match without the persistent spectre of VAR has been liberating and you can see the referees have felt free to make decisions on instinct.

The whole experience of knowing a contest cannot be re-refereed has been better for all. It will not catch on, sadly, because too many people have gone too far down the road with VAR, but we should embrace the purity of “old school football” while we can.

Evertonians – the Prime Minister included – certainly embraced the buffer of a second goal and the fact Hackney scored it also felt like a throwback: a player who was plucked out of the Championship in the summer and encouraged by Moyes to show he could make the next step up.

He did that and so, too, did Everton, their progress was assured in the 69th minute when Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall dashed down the left and crossed for Barry to finish. Moyes puffed out his cheeks as it hit the net. He, more than most, deserved to enjoy this one.
