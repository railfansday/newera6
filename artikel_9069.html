How Surface Materials Shape Contemporary Interiors

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Whether in residential interiors, workplaces, hospitality environments, or public spaces, the relationship between aesthetic intentions and technical performance can redefine how surfaces are used and how they perform. A material selected for its specific color, texture, or finish must remain visually coherent when applied to furniture fronts, cabinetry, countertops, and other interior elements. Yet how can architects and designers create visually distinctive and tactilely engaging interiors while preserving their original design intentions across different applications?

Exploring the Relationship Between Visual Depth and Tactile Experience

Material perception extends beyond what the eye can recognize. The interaction between light, texture, reflectivity, and touch contributes to the atmosphere of an interior space, influencing how surfaces convey depth, warmth, and material character. In this context, matte finishes have become an architectural resource that offers a restrained visual expression while allowing variations in texture and color to define spatial relationships.

From Material Selection to a Consistent Architectural Language

While individual finishes establish a project's material identity, maintaining that identity across different architectural components presents another challenge. A surface specified for cabinetry may require a different substrate or technical configuration when used for countertops, furniture bodies, or edging. EGGER's Decor Match system coordinates decorative finishes across different product formats. Depending on the selected design, architects can specify lacquered MDF boards, particleboards, laminates, worktops, and matching ABS edging, supporting continuity between vertical and horizontal applications. The EGGER Decorative Collection 26+ includes 25 solid-color decors, eight of which are new additions, available in coordinated PM PerfectSense Premium Matt solutions for lacquered MDF boards, laminates, and ABS edging, alongside TM PerfectSense Matt particleboard options.

Material coordination allows designers to select products according to their intended function without necessarily changing the established visual language. Cabinet fronts, furniture bodies, and horizontal surfaces can therefore respond to different technical requirements while contributing to a coherent interior composition. Beyond aesthetics, consistency also depends on predictability throughout the design and construction process. Industrial manufacturing supports reproducible surface quality, while access to samples, technical information, and supporting services helps architects evaluate finishes before finalizing specifications. Long-term decor availability further contributes to planning reliability, particularly for larger developments or projects implemented in successive phases.
