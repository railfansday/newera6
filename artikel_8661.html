Open QuicksilverOne from Capital One if you have fair credit, want no security deposit, and will charge more than $2,600 a year, the point where its 1.5% cash back covers the $39 annual fee. If you’ll spend less, the no-fee Capital One Platinum is the cheaper card for building credit.

Capital One offers the QuicksilverOne to people with fair credit, which FICO puts at scores of 580 to 669, and asks no security deposit. If you list no assets, your monthly income must be at least $425 more than your rent or mortgage payment, or $215 more for students.

The QuicksilverOne suits fair-credit borrowers who charge more than $2,600 a year, pay in full, and don’t want to put down a deposit. Borrowers who charge less build credit for no annual fee on the Capital One Platinum, and those whose credit already qualifies for the Quicksilver get the same 1.5% without the fee.
