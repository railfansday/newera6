Tom Condon is the fairly new Blazers owner, and he already is reviled in Portland for some penny-pinching moves and letting coach Tiago Splitter leave after he led the team to a surprise Western Conference playoff spot last season. Splitter was quickly snapped up by the Bulls. Condon then made the unprecedented decision to hire his full-time replacement, former Timberwolves assistant Micah Nori, on a deal with just the first season guaranteed. The second and third years feature team options that are held by the franchise, so Condon and the front office have all the leverage.

One wonders why Nori even took this job, but there are only 32 of them and this looks like a team on the rise. And boy is it a fun team now with the additions of Morant and future Hall of Famer and franchise icon Damian Lillard. It's only the second time in the last 15 seasons that the Blazers have opened a season 3-0.

Morant's value was at an all-time low in Memphis when he was acquired in June for forwards Jerami Grant and Kris Murray in a steal. When Morant is healthy and doesn't have off-court nonsense surrounding him, he's arguably a top-20 player in the league and definitely top 10 for most exciting. The 27-year-old has happily fit in as a team player, not trying to dominate the ball, and he's averaging 16.3 points on 58.8% shooting from the field and 6.3 assists. For tonight, he's set at O/U 24.5 points + assists and the model has him at 18.4 points and 5.9 helpers.

Lillard was on the Blazers last season but missed it due to an injury from his final year with Milwaukee. The 36-year-old looks great, putting up 21.7 points, 4.3 rebounds and 4.0 assists per night. What team has a more interesting backcourt than Morant and Lillard? Plus, two good backups in Jrue Holiday and Scott Henderson, and I haven't even mentioned arguably the best all-around player on the team in forward Deni Avdija (22.3 PPG, 7.0 RPG, 6.3 APG).

Portland leads the NBA in scoring at a whopping 129.3 points per game. It also leads in made field goals per game (45.7), and assist/turnover ratio (2.24). Last season, it went 21-22 in clutch-time games, which is when the score is within five points in the final five minutes. So far this year, 2-0.

Is the team ready to win the West? Probably not, as it's priced at +2800 and has a season wins total of 45.5, but the Blazers could make some noise. No doubt the guy carrying the Grizzlies has been journeyman guard Ty Jerome, the reigning Western Conference Player of the Week. His career scoring average is 9.7 PPG, and that's up a few notches as he has posted a whopping 29.8 PPG number so far – Jerome is the +600 leader for NBA Most Improved Player. Jerome leads the NBA in second-half scoring average (19.0) and fourth-quarter average (10.0) among players who have played a minimum of three games. You are already hearing that teams are calling the Grizzlies about him. He's set at O/U 24.5 points here.

Might as well sell high on the 29-year-old, who has a player option for next season and is making only $9.2 million this year. Jerome isn't part of the team's future, which is built around the likes of rookie Cameron Boozer (+220 ROY favorite), second-year Cedric Coward and third-year but injury-prone big man Zach Edey, along with a few others.

I do think Memphis is playing quite a bit over its head, as its four opponents all are well under .500 and the Grizz also have caught some injury breaks on their foes – like in Monday's 110-105 win at Utah.
