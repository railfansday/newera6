Red Bull’s Newest Ski Film Is Made By Pros—But Is Surprisingly For Everyone

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

(Photo: Courtesy of Hai Yen / Legs of Steel / Red Bull Content Pool)

Unless all of your friends are die-hard skiers, you’ve probably endured the unfortunate experience of trying to explain ski films. For those used to tidy narratives and strong plots, it can be hard to see why a film that relies mostly on epic drone photography and loose plot development is worth several hours of someone’s time.

Thankfully, now you don’t have to. Filmmaker and pro skier Paddy Graham and his motley crew have done it for you. In his newest film, “We Can’t Not,” Graham writes a love letter to his favorite format, the ski movie. It’s also a message to his family, who appear in the film. The premise: This is Graham’s last hoorah, and before he steps away from the wild (and dangerous) world of steep skiing, he wants to help his family understand what exactly making a ski film entails.

“Paddy has been asked a lot about what he actually does, and why,” Red Bull Media House executive producer Sarah Stow told SKI. The film is essentially a response to the question Graham is trying to answer: Why keep making ski films? Why keep skiing such big lines? Why do you even do this? He can’t not.

The film opens up as many others do: a montage of big mountain skiing set to rock music, a few yew soundbites, and close-up shots of freeride jackets with sponsor logos. Then it takes a turn. We meet our protagonist and start to see the film’s thesis unfurl.

That’s the first moment that I realized this film was going to be different.

The idea of answering a legitimate question is what separates “We Can’t Not” from other films: the journey that the audience follows as Graham sits around a dinner table trying to explain to his children and parents what he does when he says he makes ski films is not unlike conversations we’ve had with our ski-film newbie friends.

The issue with many ski films is they’re created for an audience that already loves to ski; in “We Can’t Not,” the audience can be anyone. One way in which Graham does this is by introducing his cast to the audience like we’re meeting for the first time. Each castmate has an intro that explains his distinct “why”: why he loves to ski and why Graham chose to feature him in the film.

“We really wanted to introduce the athletes so audiences connect with them,” Stow explained, “which is such a smart move in a sport where we often only see goggles and helmets.”

The four other skiers: Kye Peterson, Markus Eder, Tom Ritsch, and Finn Bilous, all complement Graham’s conceit: They’re all a little maverick themselves.

But really, the story isn’t about the group of irreverent skiers—it’s about what compels a group of irreverent skiers to do something as outrageous as shooting another ski film. And, for that matter, why ski films ought to keep existing into the next generation. After all, Instagram and TikTok are full of more short-form ski clips than one could ever get through.

The response, at least according to these diehards? A passion for the sport that runs several standard deviations deeper than your average weekend warrior.

A point made clear by the team’s decision to rent out Hyperbowl, a VR film studio outside Munich that, according to Stow, is “more used to Hollywood productions than a bunch of pro skiers turning up.”

At Hyperbowl, a studio outside Munich that Stow says is “more used to Hollywood productions than a bunch of pro skiers turning up,” the cast found that sometimes the most radical lines exist many miles away from the mountain. The Alaska backdrops had to be scanned on location in advance, and Stow says it was the studio’s first indoor mountain ski shoot. Her verdict: the team was “pushing innovation in this film, not just taking the classic safe route.”

The film’s other big swing is its narrator. Sean Bean, a voice that Game of Thrones and Lord of the Rings fans may recognize, narrates the film. Stow says Bean, despite not being a skier, got so into the project that he became a method voice actor. For one of the final scenes, where he tells the audience the film isn’t over yet, she says he ran in from another room to get the tone right.

Ultimately, the meta-filmmaking in “We Can’t Not” helps to underscore the point of not only their ski film, but all ski films: If you love something deeply enough, no lark seems crazy.

While the film could have used a few female voices—there are no female skiers in the film, though many women lined the production teams for Red Bull — the bigger lesson of “We Can’t Not” resonates in its pure obsession with the art form.

For those who’ve struggled to wrangle their non-skier friends to the theaters, there is a story worth seeing here. For you and for them.

You can watch “We Can’t Not” for free on Red Bull TV, and in select theaters across the world.
