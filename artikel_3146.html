Apple Sales Rise 12%; Tariff Refunds Pad EPS: Rapid Earnings Reaction

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

We look at how much of Apple's earnings growth came from the business itself, and what its ambitious holiday lineup has to prove.

Apple (AAPL -1.83%) posted record September-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion for its fiscal 2026 fourth quarter, up 12% from a year ago. Shares were roughly flat in after-hours trading. The sales growth is real, but part of the profit gain is not. Tariff refunds added about a point of gross margin and $0.05 to earnings per share, and without them, earnings grew a bit slower than sales.

The refunds are a one-time item, so they belong outside the trend. Take them out, and EPS growth drops to about 11% by our math, a hair below sales growth. Apple's steady buybacks usually push per-share earnings ahead of sales. Trailing sales instead suggests underlying costs kept pace with revenue this quarter.

The bigger test is the holiday quarter. Apple heads into December with the iPhone Duo, new home hardware, touch on the Mac, and a rebuilt Siri. A launch this broad can pull forward upgrades. But new designs often cost more to build early in their run, which can squeeze margin just as the refund help fades.

Watch gross margin next quarter without refunds. Holding near this quarter's underlying level of about 47% would show the new lineup isn't eating into profit. Also listen for December-quarter guidance on today's call, since the release gives none.
