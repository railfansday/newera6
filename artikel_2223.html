Why Jeff Stelling will have one eye on Sheffield Wednesday this season

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Sheffield Wednesday have confirmed Dave Challinor as their new manager, and with that appointment will come plenty of intrigue from League One fans and Jeff Stelling over how they'll cope with him in charge during the remainder of their campaign.

It seemed inevitable that, as soon as Simon Wilson left Stockport County to become Sheffield Wednesday's new Sporting Director, the ex-Hatters boss would be following suit.

Both departed Edgeley Park in the summer, having been key parts of their rise from the National League to the brink of the Championship after back-to-back third-placed finishes in League One. Now, they'll be looking to get to the second tier together in South Yorkshire.

The Wednesday job is a big one, but it's one that a lot of people agree Challinor deserves to take on, having experienced success everywhere he's been, from the Northern Premier League to the EFL, including at Hartlepool United, of which Stelling is an Honorary President.

Jeff Stelling is an iconic football television and radio presenter, and his voice has been associated with his boyhood club, Hartlepool United, for many years.

The 71-year-old has been an Honorary President at the club for over a decade now, having originally been appointed in 2015, before resigning in protest of then-owner Raj Singh in May 2025 and being re-appointed by new owner Landon Smith back in February.

It's been a real up-and-down 10 years at Victoria Park. Back in the 2016/17 campaign, the Monkey Hangers were relegated out of the Football League for the first time in their history. The man to take them back four years later was Dave Challinor.

Hartlepool had finished no higher than 12th in their first three seasons in the National League. But in his first full campaign at the club, Challinor led them to a play-off position (as he's become known to do in full seasons) and beat Torquay United on penalties in the final to return to the EFL.

It allowed Challinor to coach in the Football League for the first time, but just three months into the 2021/22 campaign, he'd leave for Stockport, whom they'd beaten in the play-off semi-final the year prior. Either way, the 51-year-old provided Stelling with plenty of great memories, and the two are still close five years on.

Ultimately, there was no bad blood from Stelling's perspective, as showcased by the fact that he has named one of his cats after the new Sheffield Wednesday manager.

In a story back in January 2021, while Challinor was still Hartlepool boss, it was revealed that the cat was welcomed into the Stelling home and named after him after he turned up on their doorstep.

And even despite the 51-year-old moving on, the cat hasn't had their name changed. Speaking on his talkSPORT breakfast show on Monday morning, Stelling spoke of Challinor's appointment at Hillsborough, predicting that he'd do a good job at his new club.

"Sheffield Wednesday have a new manager — my mate, Dave Challinor, and not Dave Challinor the cat; Dave Challinor the manager, obviously. I do have a cat called Dave Challinor," he joked.

"A terrific appointment, and one I feel will do a brilliant job there, because he’s done a brilliant job wherever he has been."

Challinor has a lot of credentials. He has a win percentage over 50% and has finished no lower than a play-off place in all 15 of his full seasons in management.

The 51-year-old has done a good job taking clubs up the footballing pyramid, which is exactly what Sheffield Wednesday want to do. You get the feeling, though, that this job will come with different pressures compared to his other roles.

We've already seen Wednesday fans turn on Henrik Pedersen quickly as soon as results started to fade. Even with four wins from five to begin the campaign, that performance in defeat against Bradford City was fixated on by a percentage of the fanbase.

Stockport were well backed over Challinor's tenure at Edgeley Park, but a lot of fans in the stands were content with being competitive and enjoying the ride they were on from the sixth tier to the third in the space of a decade.

That romance won't be there at Hillsborough. This is a club and a fanbase that are demanding immediate success, and it'll be a real test of Challinor's managerial credentials in front of 30,000 loud Wednesday fans and sold-out away ends each week.

But there's a lot of confidence that he'll be able to thrive in this environment, and not just from Jeff Stelling. If he can lead Wednesday back to the Championship and maybe even beyond, perhaps there'll be a few more cats called Dave Challinor in South Yorkshire.
