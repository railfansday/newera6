Put a big purchase you’ll pay off over months on a card with a 0% intro period. The Chase Freedom Unlimited suits most buyers, adding cash back and purchase protection, while the U.S. Bank Shield gives the most time at 0%. If you’ll pay in full, pick the card that earns most where you buy.

Start by working out whether you can pay the purchase off before a card’s 0% interest period ends. Divide the price by the months the period lasts. A $3,000 sofa takes about $143 a month to pay off in 21 months, or $200 a month in 15. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau warns that the minimum payment usually won’t clear a promotional balance in time, so set fixed autopay for that monthly amount. During a card’s 0% period the purchase costs no interest, and whatever is left when it ends is charged interest only from then on. Store financing that waives interest only if you pay in full is deferred interest instead, which bills you all the interest since the day you bought if any balance remains when the promotion ends.

If you’ll pay the bill in full, the 0% period matters less than what the card earns and covers. Some cards here pay 5% or more in a category you choose, and purchase protection pays to repair or replace a new item that’s stolen or damaged within 90 to 120 days of purchase. Either way, apply shortly before you shop. The 0% period starts counting when the account opens, and the credit limit you’re approved for decides whether the purchase fits.
