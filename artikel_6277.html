Lufthansa Now Requires An Account Just To Check In Online

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Buying a ticket and having your booking confirmation will no longer be enough to check in online for many Lufthansa Group flights. The airline group is making a Travel ID account mandatory, adding another requirement to a process that should be simple. A major expansion takes effect today, with departures from the United States following later this month.

According to Lufthansa Group’s notice to travel partners, passengers will increasingly need a Travel ID to check in through the websites and apps of Lufthansa, SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, Air Dolomiti, Edelweiss, and Discover Airlines.

The rollout is phased by departure point:

One person can check in multiple travelers using their own Travel ID. Someone assisting another passenger may also use their own account for personal assistance.

Lufthansa presents Travel ID as a convenient way to use one login across its airlines, with stored information and fewer forms to complete. It also promotes personalized offers.

Certainly, a frequent Lufthansa Group passenger would likely find that useful. If you regularly fly Lufthansa, SWISS, and Austrian, having your information available across those airlines can save the annoying hassle of have to re-enter personal details.

A booking reference and surname have long been enough to retrieve a reservation and begin check-in. If additional identity or travel-document checks are necessary, an airline can request those during that process. I don’t understand why creating an account must now be part of obtaining a boarding pass, other than for the collection of data.

I already have more travel accounts than I care to count, but count me in here…I already have a Lufthansa Travel ID and I welcome this. But for an occasional traveler who simply wants to check in for a flight, it creates another step with another password or login process to manage.

Passengers who do not want an account can still check in at the airport. Good, but it does not make the online requirement particularly customer-friendly. Online check-in lets passengers helps passengers save time. Someone traveling with only a carry-on may have no reason to visit a check-in counter at all.

Why make that person choose between opening an account and completing check-in at the airport?

Airlines have spent years encouraging passengers to handle more tasks themselves. That works best when the digital process is easy to use. Adding an enrollment requirement seems like an odd way to improve it.

The exception for tickets issued by other airlines is also worth highlighting….an odd disincentive to book away, it seems to me.

Lufthansa Group is expanding its mandatory Travel ID requirement for online check-in throughout November, including US departures beginning November 16.

I have no objection to a shared account that makes traveling across the group easier. But passengers should be able to decide whether they want one. Buying a ticket ought to be enough to access the basic tools needed to use it like online check-in…
