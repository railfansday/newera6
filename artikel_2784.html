Brian Sabean Humbled by Hall Of Fame Bid

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Former Giants general manager Brian Sabean knows he is not the biggest name on his Hall of Fame ballot. He told reporters on Monday that he is the only person on the list who is not a true icon in the sport. This honesty stands out in a game full of loud voices and big egos. Sabean built three championship teams in San Francisco, yet he still feels like the underdog among his peers.

The New York Post reports that Sabean joined the Class of 2027 Contemporary Baseball Era ballot. He was one of eight people selected for this special group. The committee is scheduled to gather in San Diego on Dec. 6 to cast their votes. A candidate must obtain 75 percent support from the 16-person panel to gain entry. Sabean knows the odds are steep, but he remains grateful for the chance.

Sabean did not try to spin his story. He called the nomination "flattering" because of the strength of the ballot. He said the group is "such an august group." His reaction was simple and direct. He told the press by phone, "I'm blown away. I'm beyond humbled." That line cuts through the usual noise of baseball politics. Most executives would claim they deserve the spot. Sabean admitted he is lucky just to be in the room.

He shares the ballot with legends like Dusty Baker and Bruce Bochy. Sabean himself hired both men to manage the Giants. The list also includes Sandy Alderson, Joe Maddon, Lou Piniella, Ted Turner, and Bill White. These names carry huge weight in the history of the game. Sabean knows he is the odd one out when it comes to fame. He told the Post, "It's flattering because of the strength of the ballot. It's such an august group. I think I'm the only person on it that's not an icon in the sport."

Sabean's path to this moment is long and winding. He spent four seasons as an executive assistant to Yankees GM Brian Cashman. The organization chose not to retain him for the 2027 season. This move ended his second tenure with the team. He first worked for the Yankees in the 1980s and 1990s. He played a major role in developing the Core Four during that time.

Before the Yankees, he helped draft and sign Derek Jeter, Andy Pettitte, and Mariano Rivera. These players formed the backbone of a dynasty. He later served as the GM for three championship Giants teams. His resume is full of wins. Yet, he is still fighting for recognition from the voters. The committee will look at his work from the late 20th century and beyond. They need to decide if his impact was enough to earn his place in Cooperstown.

Sabean's approach is a breath of fresh air. He does not demand respect. He earns it by admitting his place in history. The voters will see his honesty in his reaction. They will see a man who built teams but does not claim to be a god. The vote on Dec. 6 will decide his fate. If he gets in, it will be a victory for the quiet builders of the game.

His story shows that you do not need to be the loudest voice to be important. Sabean helped create legends. He hired managers who won World Series titles. He found the players who changed the game. Even if he does not get the 75 percent needed, his legacy remains. The ballot itself proves his worth. He is among the best, even if he does not say so.
