Two years ago, we shared the sensitively renewed “bush modernist” home of architect Brad Mitchell and interior designer Kerli Valk—a thoughtful renovation that won a 2023 Australian Interior Design Awards Commendation and a 2023 Houses Award for Sustainability. Now the couple, founders of Melbourne-based Nüüd Studio, have produced another award-winning design by following the same successful formula: maximize natural light, create functional flow, focus on exquisite joinery. And, above all else, where possible, make the most of what’s already there.

“One of the most effective ways to manage a budget is to work with the existing character and materials of a building rather than trying to contradict them,” says Kerli. Here, she expounds on the idea.

“When we first saw the property, much of the interior was a combination of pine lining boards, bagged brick, and whitewashed timber ceilings. At the time it felt quite dark and dated, and understandably it was difficult for the clients to imagine how those elements could form part of a new, more contemporary interior.

“Rather than removing them, we looked at how color, texture, and light could change the way they were perceived. The existing ceilings were left largely untouched, the brick walls were celebrated rather than concealed, and the pine-clad walls were painted.

“Retaining these elements was probably one of the largest savings in the project, but it also gave the finished interior much more character. We are often more interested in transforming what is already there than replacing it. Sometimes a relatively light intervention—a change in color, texture or the way light reaches a surface—can completely alter how an existing material is experienced.”
