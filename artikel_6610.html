Carne Guisada

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Recipe by Margaret Knoebel, Milwaukee, Wisconsin

With its warm spices and a rich gravy, this slow-braised carne guisada is perfect in tacos or on a bed of rice.

Carne guisada, a classic Mexican beef stew whose name translates to “stewed meat,” has a rich culinary backstory. Its roots stretch across northern Mexico and into Texas, where slow-braised beef dishes are a staple of home kitchens, taquerias and Tex-Mex restaurants alike. Tender beef simmers in a chili-based sauce, then is often served as a taco filling with soft flour tortillas. Some versions (especially in Texas) include potatoes to make it a one-pot meal. Others focus solely on the fall-apart meat and the smooth, peppery sauce built from dried chiles. You win either way.

For our carne guisada recipe, we keep the signature slow-cooked, deeply seasoned beef and rich gravy, but say yes to the potatoes for extra body and comfort. The use of beer, Worcestershire sauce and tomato paste brings layered depth that complements the traditional spices like cumin and chili powder. We aren’t claiming this is the only version because, honestly, that doesn’t exist. But this dish absolutely captures the spirit of classic carne guisada: tender meat, bold flavor and something meant to be shared.

In a large bowl, toss the beef with 1/4 teaspoon of salt. In a separate large bowl, combine the flour and paprika. Add the beef to the flour a few pieces at a time, tossing to coat them.

In a Dutch oven, heat the oil over medium heat. Brown the beef in batches, adding additional oil as necessary. Remove the pieces with a slotted spoon.

Editor’s Tip: Those browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot are called fond, and they’re flavor gold. Don’t even think about washing them away.

To the same pan, add the onion. Cook and stir until it’s tender, three to five minutes. Stir in the tomato, jalapeno and garlic, and cook until they’re fragrant and the color starts to darken slightly.

Add the browned beef back to the pan. Add the beer, and bring it to a boil, scraping the bottom to release the browned bits. Simmer until the liquid is reduced by half, about five minutes.

Stir in the broth, Worcestershire sauce, crushed red pepper flakes, chili powder, cumin, bay leaf and the remaining 1/2 teaspoon of salt, and bring the mixture to a boil. Reduce the heat, then cover the pot and simmer the stew until the meat is almost tender, about 1 hour and 30 minutes.

Add the potatoes and red wine vinegar. Cover the pot and simmer the stew until the meat and vegetables are tender, about 30 minutes longer.

Discard the bay leaf. Using the back of a spoon or ladle, slightly mash the potatoes. If desired, shred the beef slightly with two forks. Serve with tortillas, rice, lime and cilantro.

Let the carne guisada cool completely before transferring it to an airtight container. Store it in the refrigerator, where the flavors will actually deepen and improve overnight. It’s one of those dishes that might taste even better the next day. Carne guisada lasts up to four days in the refrigerator.

Carne guisada freezes very well. Store it in freezer-safe containers for up to three months, and thaw it overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.

Reheat carne guisada gently on the stovetop over medium-low heat until it’s warmed through. Add a splash of broth if the sauce has thickened too much.

You can use beef shoulder, bottom round or beef stew meat for carne guisada. The key is choosing a tougher cut with connective tissue that breaks down during the long, slow cooking time. This gives you that tender, fall-apart texture.

Whether you serve carne guisada on tortillas or rice, it also pairs beautifully with black beans, fresh salsa or even a side of warm cornbread.
