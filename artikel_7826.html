Decluttering the garage is a crucial step toward creating a safe, accessible, and high-functioning extension of your home. Because a garage serves as the main entryway for many households—as well as the storage hub for heavy tools, seasonal gear, sports equipment, lawn and garden supplies, and more—an unorganized space can quickly become an eyesore and safety hazard that makes daily routines frustrating and hard to maintain.

As professional organizer Abriana Sanford, founder of Sorted and Styled in Atlanta, Georgia, notes, “Garages tend to become the catch-all space for anything that doesn’t have a designated home. Over time, this can make it difficult to find what you need, but decluttering allows you to reassess what you’re storing, eliminate unnecessary items, and reclaim valuable square footage.” By turning a disorderly pile into a streamlined, easy-to-maintain setup using strategic organization solutions, you can eliminate everyday hassle, restore peace of mind, and ensure that every tool and bin serves a clear, functional purpose. Sanford shares her garage organization tips below so you can get the most out of your garage storage space.

The first step in any garage organization project is to assess what you have and to get rid of what you no longer need. “One of the most common mistakes homeowners make is purchasing storage products before evaluating what they actually need to store,” says Sanford. She recommends first sorting belongings into categories. Then eliminate duplicates and materials that you no longer need. Finally, take inventory of what’s remaining. “This allows you to create a system that works for your belongings, rather than trying to make your belongings fit a particular system.”

Once you narrow down what you have, spend some time grouping belongings by how they’re used, such as lawn care, sports equipment, tools, automotive supplies, beach and pool gear, and seasonal decorations. “Creating designated zones makes it easier to locate items and return them to their proper places,” says Sanford. She also recommends considering the frequency of use when determining placement. “Everyday essentials should be easily accessible, while seasonal belongings can occupy higher or less convenient storage areas.”

Garage floor space is valuable, especially when you park your vehicles inside, so choose a variety of storage solutions that use open areas on the walls and ceiling. “Wall-mounted shelving, pegboards, overhead racks, and vertical tool organizers allow homeowners to maximize storage without sacrificing usable floor space,” Sanford explains.

When choosing the right organizational tool, be sure to consider the weight capacity of shelving, wall-mounted systems, and overhead racks. “Heavier belongings are generally best stored on lower shelves, while lighter, less frequently used items can occupy higher storage areas,” notes Sanford. Always follow the manufacturer’s installation and load requirements.

It’s also important to consider how flexible and adaptable storage products are. “I encourage homeowners to think beyond their immediate storage needs. Adjustable shelving, movable racks, and modular systems provide flexibility as hobbies, equipment, and family needs change,” notes Sanford. As household needs evolve, the garage should be able to evolve with them. “For example, a family storing strollers and toddler toys today may need additional space for bicycles, sports equipment, or recreational gear in a few years.”

One of Sanford’s favorite approaches to garage organization is incorporating adjustable shelving and modular storage systems. Its clean, streamlined structure also provides a modern appearance. Another idea is to make the most efficient use of drawer space by either buying or making dividers. You can also add labeled stackable bins to open shelves or closed cabinetry to maximize storage.

Convenience is a core component of organizing a garage. Storage placement should reflect how frequently an item is used, says Sanford. “Homeowners shouldn’t have to move multiple bins or climb a ladder every time they need something,” she explains. “The more convenient a system is, the more likely everyone in the household will maintain it.”

Plastic storage bins with tight-sealing lids are the best choice to protect items. “In humid climates, moisture can be particularly problematic,” notes Sanford. She recommends using appropriate moisture-resistant storage containers and keeping belongings elevated off the floor when possible. “Clear, durable bins with secure-fitting lids also help protect belongings from dust and debris, while allowing homeowners to identify contents quickly.”

Sanford suggests avoiding cardboard boxes for long-term garage storage, especially if pests are a concern. “Cardboard can absorb moisture and provide hiding places for pests, so durable plastic containers with secure lids are often a better alternative.”
