Why November's +80% History Is a Statistical Flaw for XRP Holders

XRP's 15.7% drop in October has brought the token to the most unpredictable crossroads of the year. Historical data from thirteen years of observations promise the market harsh November volatility. However, behind the optimistic figures of past years lies a serious methodological flaw capable of disorienting investors amid the current pressure.

The main trap for the market is the asymmetry of returns. According to Cryptorank, XRP's average November gain is an impressive +80.2%, but this figure is completely distorted by two isolated anomalies: a surge of +531.9% in 2013 and a rally of +281.7% in 2024. The month's median, a negative -10%, reflects reality much more accurately.

Historically, in most cycles, XRP closed November in the red. The fourth quarter, which so far consists of October alone, is already down -16.3%, in line with the asset's seasonal tendency toward correction that the averaged statistics mask.

The median is not a perfect guide either: thirteen observations are a small sample, and the market structure has changed. Since spot XRP ETFs launched in the US in mid-November 2025, the token has had an institutional demand channel that did not exist in earlier cycles.

Mathematical pessimism is reinforced by the real balance of power on the market at the end of October. The XRP price has fallen below the key support line of $1.30 and is trading around $1.25–$1.26, marking a local downtrend.

All the upward momentum built on investors' expectations has faded, opening the way for profit-taking by short-term players. Notably, the October decline follows a 48% gain in the third quarter, XRP's best Q3 in four years, which suggests that at least part of the current pressure is profit-taking after the rally rather than seasonality alone.

The situation is aggravated by an inevitable influx of supply amid a liquidity shortage. On Sunday, November 1, a scheduled unlock of 1 billion XRP from Ripple's escrow wallets will take place.

It is worth noting, however, that the escrow unlock is a routine monthly event that has followed the same schedule since 2017, and Ripple typically returns 600–800 million XRP to new escrow contracts. The real net addition to circulating supply is therefore closer to 200–400 million XRP.

Even so, against the backdrop of thin liquidity, this volume is enough to put additional pressure on the price, as it will enter the market simultaneously with macroeconomic pressure and the aftermath of the recent wave of forced liquidations of long positions worth more than $1.29 billion, caused by a security incident at the Binance exchange.

At the same time, the wave of liquidations has partially cleared excess leverage from the market. If funding rates stay negative and open interest does not recover, the bearish scenario may be capped by a short squeeze, which would make a retest of $1.30–$1.32 more likely than the statistics suggest.
