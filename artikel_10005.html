Haas: Postgres 19 is late, and it's AI's fault

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

The long-term picture is less clear and in some ways more troubling: given that we have forty years of bugs that humans were unable to find but LLMs are quite capable of discovering, how are we ever going to catch up, and how will PostgreSQL continue to innovate? Right now, I'm not sure. What I do know is that I, and many others, are working really hard to figure it out.

If Postgres 19 comes out with less new fancy features but far higher quality, that's a huge "selling" point in my mind. And I don't know about Postgres' policies, but you're not limited to using AI just to find bugs, it can also be used to fix them, write tests, etc. That'd be of help with the catching up.

In my personal opinion, the actual title should have been "Postgres 19 is late, and we're thankful to AI for all the bug fixes".

The fault is insuffient quality of software produced, as AI has raised the bar for quality ny a significant amount. The promise (it seems) is/was faster development of new features, however due to the side effect of much better QA done by AI, the development has slowed down instead. This is not unique to Postgres, it's not unique to Open Source software, it's everywhere. Countless projects and org are now very busy fixing yesterday problems with no time to create shiny new features.

Maybe we will find all of them in a year or so, and also catch new bugs during development before release. Projects have to figure out the workflow, but the overall result may be a net positive.

It's AI's fault if the reports are of poor quality, even if there is an underlying, real bug. "Poor quality" can cover a multitude of sins, including burying the relevant information in, quite plainly, too many f-ing words with no indication of which parts are important and which parts are not. That generates real work and chasing bogus leads is very exhausting. If only the report would say "In liboink v1.2.3, your allocation at foo.c:1234 is one byte to short. Sample program, stack trace, and suggested patch attached." Facts, not speculation. And a patch needs to (1) apply, (2) not contain old commented-out code, (3) not contain comments about the old code, (4) follow the coding style of the project it's intended for, (5) not try to fix or change anything unrelated. If I have to edit the patch, that patch isn't very helpful.
