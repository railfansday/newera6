Pagani Huayra 70 Anima Shows Off in Vegas With Diamond Dust-Infused Paint

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

The sparkle you’re seeing in the Inky Blues paint comes from real diamond dust.

In case you were looking for inspo on how to spec your next vehicle purchase, consider the Pagani Huayra 70 Anima, which was just revealed at the Concours at Wynn Last Vegas ... complete with diamond-dust-infused paint.

If a paint were capable of calling others poor, this would be the one to do it. Typically, metallic paints are made by mixing tiny aluminum flakes with the paint to imbue that familiar metallic look. But why not use diamond dust to create the metallic effect of your dreams? That’s what the owner of the Huayra 70 Anima did, and while Pagani did not detail the cost of doing so, it had to be astronomical.

This particular Huayra is the third in Pagani’s “Grani Complicazioni” collection, alongside the Trionfo and Derecho. The blue color you’re seeing here is called Inky Blues, and inside you’ll notice a blue and white two-tone interior. Pagani also points out that the stripes are made of platinum leaf, so being ostentatious was clearly part of the brief. Further evidence of that is seen in the bespoke exhaust system, which is made of white ceramic.

“Anima” means soul in Italian, and while Pagani didn’t explain the reasoning behind the name, there’s no shortage of soul on tap here. This Huayra is one of only a few equipped with a seven-speed manual transmission, and the exposed linkage and artful three pedals are proudly displayed in photos. There’s no roof to be found on the roadster body style. Inside, a “Huayra 70 Anima” plaque displays Horacio Pagani’s signature and declaration of this car being three-of-three in the collection.

That 7-speed manual comes connected, as with all stick shift Huayras, to a Mercedes-AMG-made twin-turbocharged 6.0-liter V-12 that cranks out 864 horsepower and 811 pound-feet of torque. All that twist goes to the rear wheels, going through an electromechanical limited-slip diff along the way.

If you were at the Concours at the Wynn Las Vegas, you’d have seen this piece of art displayed alongside the other Grani Complicazioni cars. And considering how rare it is (and how hard diamond paint might be to take care of), who knows when we’ll see it next in public ... although we imagine the where could well be Pebble Beach.
