Cornell prevails on DEI hiring lawsuit after judge finds plaintiff ‘had no intention of reentering academia’

The ruling sidestepped arguments about diversity-based discrimination, which have gained some traction in the federal court system.

“In situations where a plaintiff is challenging a discriminatory hiring process or program and there is a reason as to why he or she is unable to apply for that program, they can establish standing to sue by demonstrating ‘that they are able and ready to apply, but a discriminatory policy prevents them from doing so on equal footing,’” the court explained.

Suddaby determined the plaintiff in Wright was “perhaps able but not ready” to apply, given statements he made on social media, in quotes provided to The Daily Caller and in a Wall Street Journal editorial. All “uniformly reference that he had left academia,” the court said.

The plaintiff also stated his disinterest in applying for roles at institutions that required DEI statements, which further eroded the case for standing, the court found.

“The documented fact that Plaintiff was so opposed to the use of any sort of DEI initiatives by academic institutions that he chose not to apply to openings with institutions that included a DEI component in their application process strongly indicates that he was not reasonably ‘ready’ to apply for employment with Defendant, an institution that had an expressly stated commitment to certain DEI initiatives,” Suddaby said.

“We are reviewing the order, and we are dedicated to continuing the fight on behalf of [the plaintiff] against unlawful discrimination,” Richard Lawson, litigation vice-chair for the America First Policy Institute, said in a comment to HR Dive.

HR Dive also reached out to Cornell for comment, but did not hear back by press time.

Due to the lack of standing, Suddaby managed to sidestep the arguments about DEI-based discrimination, which have gained some traction in both the federal court system and at the nation’s top equal employment opportunity enforcement arm.

DEI-based discrimination — mainly against majority-group plaintiffs — has also been a central focus of the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission during the second Trump administration. The New York Times and Harvard University are among the high-profile targets EEOC has pursued as it has sought to investigate DEI’s allegedly discriminatory impact on hiring practices.
