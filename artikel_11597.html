Bored of Your Bathroom? Try One of These Four Design Trends

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

From monastic minimalism to juicy orange tones, here are a few big ideas for the bathroom.

Dive into the most innovative ideas for the bathroom, from the color of the season (it’s seriously bold) to looks that increase light or maximize zen.

First in vogue in the 1930s and then the ’80s, this is a look that loves a comeback (never more so than in the bathroom).

New York-based Darren Jett, founder of Jett Projects, refers to the glass-brick-clad wetroom in this SoHo apartment (pictured top) as a “voyeuristic bathing experience.” The floating marble vanity set against a floor-to-ceiling mirror increases that sensual, peekaboo quality. jettprojects.com

Glass bricks separate this artistic shower space, with its mosaic by Trufle Mozaiki, commissioned by Polish design studio Obiekty. The result is light that appears to dance with the same freedom as the depicted figure. obiekty.studio

Studio Karhard is the design team behind the look of legendary Berlin nightclub Berghain—the club that famously doesn’t have clocks so people won’t know the time of day. Here, it distilled its aesthetic into a more homely setting with industrial elements and curved glass bricks that bring an unexpected amount of light. karhard.de

Colored glass is an exciting take on the look: American design studio Charlap Hyman & Herrero boosted the sunshine in this Silver Lake home with an amber glow. ch-herrero.com

Take a sprinkling of brutalism, add a note of high-end spa chic and you have this pared-back aesthetic.

Belgian architect Nicolas Schuybroek is renowned for his minimalist approach—his studio has designed Aesop stores in Milan and London—so the calm simplicity of this bathroom in an Antwerp office block is true to form, with a font-like bespoke marble basin. ns-architects.com

It was the idea of the tokonoma (a recessed alcove in traditional Japanese homes for displaying art and treasured objects) that was the starting point for the form of this monolithic bathroom design in a Berlin apartment. Carved from Turkish marble, it was conceived by Studio Loes and presents functional bathing areas as moments of discovery. studio-loes.com

Tadelakt walls in a mineral-rich tone lend this compact shower nook in Mosley Thorold’s Clerkenwell project an atmospheric, even spiritual quality. mosleythorold.com

Here, design studio Kerstens chose materials that spoke to the beauty of this home’s French coastal setting. From the fossil-dotted muschelkalk limestone flooring to the Studio Loho bathtub, it’s a quiet reflection on texture. kerstens.be

Love an uplifting shower in the morning? This color will give you the vitamin boost you crave.

When choosing a color as bold as this shade of orange, it can pay to keep everything else simple. In this design by architect Caleb Mulvena from Studio Mapos, the “Isabella” tiles from DDS Tile are arranged in a precise grid and paired with white sanitaryware. studiomapos.com

For this bathroom in London Paddington’s Grand Hotel Bellevue, designer Fabrizio Casiraghi sought to pair a traditionally British style with an unexpected pop of orange. It’s a unique but complementary combination—the black-tiled picture rail bisects the walls, directing the gaze to the elaborately framed still-life painting. fabriziocasiraghi.com

Speaking of unexpected combinations: see this lavender-and-orange guest bathroom by Persistent Objects Interiors. From the lilac grout dividing the crackle finish tiles to the accessories, it’s a pairing that just works. persistentobjectsny.com

Doldam House in South Korea’s Seoraksan National Park by architecture firm One Aftr is a vision in orange that’s reflected in the two-tone Palet tiles that line its bathroom. one-aftr.com

The timber with the highly patterned grain continues its dominance with a takeover of the bathroom.

A collaboration between architects Agathe Lavaud of Agence Volta and Martin Massé, this Parisian home sees the pair lean into the property’s 1970s heritage with their use of materials. In the bathroom, that means travertine-topped, stormy burr wood, whose pattern is reflected in a mirror-clad wall. volta-architecture.com; martin-masse.com

Crafted from elm-root wood (a timber with a naturally intriguing burr pattern) and Chinese marble, the vanity unit in this Madrid apartment by Plutarco manages to marry two very impactful materials deftly. The terrazzo floor continues the pattern-maxing. plutarco.design

This home’s high ceilings are used to their fullest effect with bespoke burr-wood joinery by Bosko Projekty stretching the full height and expanding to form a vanity. A mirrored wall extends the space even further. bo-sko.pl

Created for Polish company Alex Concierge, this office bathroom is more memorable than most, thanks to Skandal Studio’s pairing of dark burr wood with a pop of Yves Klein blue to create a bespoke two-tone vanity unit. skandalstudio.com
