Man Jailed for 14 Months After Stealing $19K Worth of Camera Gear From Photographer

A man has been sentenced to 14 months in prison for robbing a photographer of nearly $19,000 worth of camera equipment as he was preparing for a photo shoot.

The unidentified photographer was preparing to carry out a photo shoot at the studio of renowned late textile designer Bernat Klein near the town of Selkirk in Scotland, U.K., on September 9 last year.

Klein’s former studio is a modernist building designed by architect Peter Womersley in 1972. Womersley was one of the greatest Brutalist architects to have worked in Scotland.

The studio stood empty for more than 20 years and fell into severe disrepair due to water damage, neglect, and vandalism. It was bought by a consortium of Scottish heritage and design organizations in July 2025 in an effort to save it from ruin.

According to a report by the Border Telegraph, 39-year-old Grant Forrest turned up at the studio as the photographer was getting ready for his photo shoot.

Forrest falsely claimed that his brother had bought the building before pursuing the photographer and repeatedly striking him on the head and body. He also accused the photographer of being from MI5 and continued behaving aggressively. A passing motorist eventually stopped to offer the photographer assistance.

Forrest then took a bag containing the photographer’s camera equipment, which had over $18,785 (£14,000) worth of gear. Forrest then drove away in the direction of Peebles, a town in Scotland, before police eventually caught up with Forrest.

Border Telegraph reports that Forrest pleaded guilty to four charges – assault and robbery, threatening or abusive behaviour, failing to co-operate with a preliminary breath test and drink-driving. Sheriff Robert Fife imposed jail sentences totalling 14 months and banned Forrest from driving for three years.

Defense lawyer Mat Patrick said his client had thrown the bag of camera equipment out of his car near the scene of the robbery. Forrest had later gone to the police to tell them where it was, but was unsure whether it had been recovered. The lawyer acknowledged that Forrest’s behavior had been “bizarre” and that the incident must have been “terrifying” for the photographer.

Patrick says Forrest had experienced numerous mental health difficulties. He explained that the incident happened during a break in his medication, which was made worse by his consumption of alcohol. When police caught up with Forrest after the theft and attack, he initially refused a preliminary alcohol or drug test before providing a urine sample that showed he was almost four times over the legal alcohol limit.
