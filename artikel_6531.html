I Tried French Fries From 11 Fast Food Chains—And One Was Clearly The Best

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

I firmly believe that French fries can make or break a fast food meal. You could have the best burger or chicken sandwich in the world, but if the fries disappoint, the whole experience takes a hit. And with so many fast food chains making fries a menu staple, you'd think they'd all have perfected them by now.

But not all French fries are created equal. Some are perfectly crispy and salty, others rely on bold seasonings to stand out, and a few are better off being covered in dipping sauce. Personally, I'm looking for fries that can stand on their own without ketchup or ranch to make them interesting. A good fry should have a crispy exterior, a fluffy potato interior, and enough seasoning to keep me reaching for another.

So I tried fries from 11 popular fast food chains, including McDonald's, Wendy's, Popeyes, and Burger King, to see which ones actually deliver. I judged each based on flavor, texture, seasoning, and overall enjoyment.

Here's how they ranked, from my least favorite to the one I'll never get tired of ordering.

I love a good crinkle-cut fry, but Raising Cane's left me seriously disappointed. The biggest issue was the texture. Rather than having a satisfying crunch on the outside, these fries were soft and limp.

The saltiness didn't help matters, either. They were overly salty, but without the crispy exterior or distinctive potato flavor I was hoping for, the seasoning didn't do much to save them.

Raising Cane's may be known for its chicken fingers and signature sauce, but based on this tasting, the fries aren't the reason I'd go back.

As much as I love Shake Shack, I was surprised by how little I enjoyed its fries.

They had a great texture, but the biggest problem was the salt. These were seasoned so heavily that they bordered on unpleasant. Instead of bringing out the potatoes' natural flavor, the salt overwhelmed everything else.

I can appreciate a generously salted fry, but there's a difference between enhancing the flavor and completely taking over. Unfortunately, Shake Shack fell into the latter category.

Chick-fil-A's waffle fries have one major advantage over the competition: their shape. The thick, waffle-cut potatoes deliver a substantial potato flavor, which I appreciated.

But as much as I enjoyed that aspect, the fries themselves weren't particularly exciting. They felt more like a way to scoop up Chick-fil-A's signature sauces than something I'd be especially eager to eat on their own.

There's nothing wrong with a fry that's great for dipping, but I wanted more flavor when eating these without sauce.

Sonic's Groovy Fries are pretty much the definition of a standard fast food French fry.

I enjoyed the crinkle-cut shape, which makes them a little more interesting than a traditional straight-cut fry. Beyond that, the flavor wasn't particularly memorable.

They're perfectly serviceable, and I certainly wouldn't complain about having them alongside a burger. But compared with some of the more flavorful fries in this ranking, Sonic didn't do quite enough to stand out.

Five Guys has a pretty distinctive flavor profile when it comes to its fries.

Because the chain cooks its fries in peanut oil, they have a noticeable peanut-oil character. That sets them apart from the competition, though I wanted a little more emphasis on the potato itself.

I did appreciate that the fries are served with the skin still on. It adds texture and a more pronounced earthy potato flavor, making them feel a little different from the more uniform fries offered by other chains.

There's plenty to like here, but the peanut-oil flavor stuck with me most.

When it comes to seasoning, Popeyes might actually have the most flavorful fries of the entire bunch.

The Cajun seasoning delivers a bold, peppery, savory flavor that immediately sets these fries apart from their competitors. There's so much more going on here than just salt, and I appreciated how much personality the seasoning brought to each bite.

Unfortunately, the texture held them back.

These fries were noticeably greasy, which made them feel heavier than I'd like. And while the seasoning was delicious, the excess oil started to take away from the experience.

Wendy's earns some serious points for letting the potato actually taste like a potato.

What I particularly loved was the skin-on preparation, which gave the fries a more pronounced, earthy potato flavor, similar to Five Guys. The skin also added texture, making each bite feel more substantial than some of the simpler fries on this list.

Sometimes you don't need a complicated seasoning blend to make a good French fry. Wendy's proves that a strong potato flavor can go a long way.

Taco Bell's Nacho Fries have always stood out from traditional fast food fries, and after trying them alongside the competition, I was reminded just how good that seasoning is.

The flavor is distinctly cheesy and savory, with a seasoning blend that makes every bite interesting. Compared with standard salted fries, these bring something completely different to the table.

My only real complaint was the texture. I wanted a little more crunch to balance out all that seasoning, especially because the flavor itself was so satisfying.

The first thing I noticed was how much larger the fries were than I expected. But what really impressed me was their combination of crispiness and potato flavor.

The exterior delivered a satisfying crunch, while the inside retained a strong, recognizable potato taste. It was exactly the kind of balance I was looking for throughout this taste test.

I went into this ranking with low expectations for Burger King's fries, but they ended up being one of the most enjoyable surprises.

KFC might not be the first chain that comes to mind when you're craving French fries, but after this tasting, I think it deserves a little more credit.

These fries had a deep, almost kettle-cooked flavor that immediately set them apart. I really enjoyed the rich potato flavor, which gave them more character than many of the other options.

The fries were wonderfully crispy, with a satisfying crunch that made them incredibly easy to keep eating. KFC delivered one of the best balances of flavor and texture in the entire lineup, and I wouldn't hesitate to order these again.

McDonald's fries have been the standard I've compared every other fast food fry to for years, and after tasting 11 different chains, they're still my favorite.

What makes McDonald's so good is how consistently they deliver the experience I want from a French fry. The flavor isn't overly complicated, but the balance of potato, salt, and that familiar fried taste just works.

While some chains lean heavily on seasoning and others struggle with texture, McDonald's gets the fundamentals right. The fries are satisfying on their own, and I can eat an entire order without getting bored.

I know what I'm getting when I order them, and that's exactly what I want.

After trying all 11 chains, I still haven't found a fast food fry that makes me want to abandon the Golden Arches.

Sometimes the obvious choice really is the best one.
