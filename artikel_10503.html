USPS is putting a permanent gas surcharge on mail and packages in 2027: What it means for your shipping costs

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

More fallout from the war in Iran: The United States Postal Service (USPS) plans to permanently add a fuel surcharge next year.

The move follows in the footsteps of both FedEx and UPS, which also increased their prices due to the skyrocketing cost of gas and diesel, according to Supply Chain Dive.

Fast Company has reached out to USPS for comment.

As of last week, gas prices were $4.22 a gallon on average in the U.S., up from $2.98 before the Iran war. Meanwhile, diesel crossed over the $6-a-gallon threshold last month and is now at $6.35 a gallon, compared with $3.76 a gallon last February.

Just to be clear, USPS previously added a flat 8% temporary surcharge back in April that expires on January 17. What’s changing is that the U.S. post office is now permanently adding a variable extra fee, meaning it will change week-to-week, per Supply Chain Dive.

At the time it filed the temporary increase, USPS cited FedEx’s 25.5% ground fuel surcharge and UPS’s 26% increase; and instead chose to tack on a fixed 8% fee for 266 days.

Here’s what the new move means for your shipping costs at the post office.

When do the new fees go into effect, and for which packages?

The changes go into effect in January 2027, when USPS will start adding an extra variable fee to packages shipped domestically as Ground Advantage, Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, and Parcel Select.

How much extra will I have to pay at the post office?

That all depends on the week. Given the volatile nature of diesel and jet fuel prices, USPS has decided to calculate the extra fees weekly, based on the fuel prices listed on the U.S. Energy Information Administration’s website. The post office is calculating the fees as a percentage of the base postage amount, according to Supply Chain Dive.

What’s the update on 2026 holiday packages and mail?

“The Postal Service performed very well during last year’s holiday season, and we are even better prepared this year for the upcoming peak mailing and shipping season,” Postmaster General David Steiner said in a statement. “We have been preparing all year for this peak season, and we have enough capacity to handle whatever comes our way.”

USPS expects its delivery will be significantly improved from last year’s holiday season, which was an improvement on the year before. In 2025, mail and packages were delivered in 2.5 days on average, compared with 2.8 days during the same period in 2024.

Each year, for the holidays, the post office sees a 40% increase in mail between October 1 and December 31.
