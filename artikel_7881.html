Some home design features have been around for generations, such as grandfather clocks, wingback chairs, and wraparound porches. Often found in drafty homes and hung only in doorways, the portiere curtain is another example dating back to the Aesthetic Movement of the late 19th century. It is named for the French word meaning “porter” or “doorkeeper.” Portieres are heavy, functional door curtains designed to block drafts, divide spaces, or close off hallways without requiring permanent walls.

The term originally referred to a heavy drapery used for doors leading into private areas, such as bedrooms or other openings in a home, says Swati Goorha, founder and principal designer of Swati Goorha Designs in New Providence, New Jersey. “These draperies were normally made from heavier materials like velvet, woolen, tapestries, etc., with design elements being created by folding and hanging them in various ways.” Portieres can create a sense of enclosure for an area but leave the doorway open, offering both privacy and an attractive look. “I really enjoy using portieres because it adds softness to architecture and does so in a nonpermanent way,” notes Goorha.

As portiere curtains make a comeback, explore how to incorporate this older feature into your home so you can enjoy the many benefits they provide.

To effectively insulate a room, portiere curtains are crafted from thick, substantial fabrics like rich velvet rather than sheer materials. Hang the portiere on a rod mounted directly above the door so it can be pushed neatly against the wall when not in use. You can also use rings inside the doorframe to ensure smooth opening and closing from both sides.

Because both sides remain visible, they are typically hung within the doorway frame so mounting looks the same from both sides. It’s also usually constructed with decorative fabrics on both faces or finished with deep hems to conceal any backing, ensuring a polished look from every angle.

This thick doorway curtain is known to provide privacy in a home. The living room, bedrooms, dining room, and hallways would all greatly benefit from portiere curtains, as they add a sense of privacy while softening the edges between rooms, according to Sheeja Nair, owner and principal designer at Raashi Design in San Ramon, California.

“The most desirable advantage of using a portiere is that you are able to create private space, such as a home office, a reading nook, or a dining room, but still be able to connect those spaces at any time by simply opening the curtains,” explains Goorha. “The visual separation created by the curtains allows for the spaces to appear to be divided yet still be used together when desired.” One reason Goorha appreciates this type of solution in older homes is because these homes often have larger openings than newer ones. In addition, the need for a swinging door may disrupt traffic flow in smaller homes where doors would otherwise be installed.While a heavy fabric provides some degree of privacy and a feeling of seclusion and enclosure, it will not provide the same level of noise reduction nor security as a solid door, warns Goorha.

Back before heating systems existed, portiere curtains were installed to help keep warmth in a room. Even today, they can still help reduce drafts by providing a physical barrier of fabric to limit the flow of air from one room into another or from entering a doorway due to its draughty condition, says Goorha. The heavier the fabric and/or more densely woven, the better the curtain will be at limiting airflow since there is less opportunity for air to move through.

“To maximize effectiveness, hang the curtain above the door opening so that sufficient fabric hangs down and covers the door opening without much space between the side edges and the floor edge,” she advises.

Portiere curtains add an extra layer of style, privacy, and elegance to any doorway, and their timelessness has led to them making a stylish comeback, says Nair. “I believe they will add warmth and texture to a home while providing a vintage touch.” However, Goorha recommends creating a new interpretation of the element rather than recreating the very ornate Victorian style of doorways. “In its purest form, a portiere feels contemporary when you use clean-line hardware, custom pleated folds, and fabric which complements your space.”
