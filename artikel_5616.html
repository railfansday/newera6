Why Waiting for Rates to Fall Can Create Other Affordability Problems

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Mortgage rates are giving homebuyers another reason to hesitate.

The average rate for a 30-year fixed mortgage reached well over 7% in October, its highest level in 13 months, according to Freddie Mac. That followed a climb from 5.98% in late February to 6.65% by Aug. 20, figures cited in a recent U.S. Bank housing-market analysis.

For a buyer, even a small rate change can alter the monthly payment considerably. Waiting for lower borrowing costs can therefore be a reasonable decision, particularly when today’s payment would leave little room for maintenance, insurance or other expenses.

However, a lower future rate does not guarantee a cheaper home purchase.

“If rates fall enough to bring more buyers back, the benefit can be partly absorbed by stronger competition,” says Jeremy Olsher, a real estate professional and Principal at Mizner Residential Group. “A buyer might secure a better rate but pay more for the property, face multiple offers or have less negotiating room.”

Additionally, lowering rates can be a sign of economic instability, including perhaps a significant response to mass unemployment and rampant inflation. While that might make homeownership more alluring, families could feel the strain in other ways, from job loss to rising grocery bills to big losses in their retirement portfolios.

Olsher recommends considering three factors before making lower rates the sole condition for buying.

Can the payment work today? Buyers should calculate the full monthly cost under current conditions, including principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, association fees and a realistic maintenance allowance. If that total is uncomfortable, the buyer may need to wait, reduce the price range or reconsider the type of property. The calculation should work without assuming that refinancing will become available later.

“Refinancing can be an option if rates eventually fall, but it should not be the reason someone accepts an unaffordable payment today,” Olsher says. “The purchase needs to stand on its own using the numbers the buyer can actually secure.”

How long does the buyer expect to stay? Transaction costs matter more when a buyer may move again within a few years. Closing costs, maintenance and future selling expenses can make a short ownership period difficult to justify, even if the mortgage payment appears manageable.

A buyer expecting to remain for several years has more time to spread those costs and absorb short-term changes in value. Someone with uncertain employment, family or relocation plans may benefit from the flexibility of renting.

Olsher says the right question is not simply whether ownership costs more than rent this month. Buyers should consider how long they expect to remain, what flexibility they need and how much cash the purchase would leave available afterward.

What does waiting cost? Delaying a purchase is not automatically costly. In some markets, renting may remain the more practical and affordable choice. Realtor.com®’s 2026 midyear forecast projected rents would decline 1.2% during the year, potentially benefiting renters in many areas.

Still, waiting can carry personal and financial costs. A household may need to renew a lease, pay moving or storage expenses, remain in an unsuitable space or postpone a relocation. The selection of available homes can also change before rates do.

“The cost of waiting is different for every buyer,” Olsher says. “For one person, another year of renting is perfectly sensible. For another, it means paying for a home that no longer fits their family while hoping the market delivers a better combination of rate, price and inventory.”

Trying to identify the perfect week to purchase gives one unpredictable number too much control over a long-term decision. Buyers can make a more grounded choice by comparing today’s full payment, the expected ownership period, available homes and the real cost of remaining where they are.

Buyers should commit when they are financially ready. If they find a home that fits their budget today, buying lets them lock in the house before prices climb further. If mortgage rates drop significantly in the future, they can refinance their loan to a lower rate without having to compete against a massive wave of new buyers.

Michael Catarevas is a senior editor for RISMedia.
