EFL club consider building new 15,000-seater stadium on old Airport site

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

Cambridge United have played their football at the Abbey Stadium since the venue opened in 1932 - but could they be making their move away to pastures new in the near future?

The U's were founded 20 years before the opening of the stadium they currently play at, with a number of pitches around the city used before the building of what is now a 7,937-capacity home.

Cambridge will soon celebrate 100 years in the Abbey Stadium in five-and-a-half years time - but will they still be there in 2032?

United are on an upward trajectory under Neil Harris, and have started life in League One rather well since promotion earlier in the year.

With an average of over 7,000 fans attending home matches, there may be the need to expand the capacity beyond what the Abbey Stadium currently holds - but it's not going to be possible at the current location.

The Abbey Stadium is tightly packed into a small location, and there's several road blocks on each side of the ground that means expansion is limited, including a lake, houses, allotments and an A-road.

It means that a different site away from their current venue would need to be found if a new stadium is going to be financially viable - have they found one already though?

As per a report from the BBC, Cambridge are already looking into future alternatives for a 15,000-capacity stadium, with a site on the soon-to-be closed Cambridge Airport a possibility.

The club have confirmed that discussions have begun with a local developer over a potential move to the site, which is based on the outskirts of the city, just like the Abbey Stadium is, with the two locations only one mile apart.

The vast area is set to be up for grabs though, with Cambridge Airport scheduled for full closure in 2029, having closed to public passenger flights 10 years ago.

United are 'exploring the feasability of the site for now', with that work expected to go on until Easter 2027, but any new stadium won't be rushed, with club chairman Godric Smith saying that he expected them to remain at the Abbey Stadium for at least another decade.

The Hill Group - who bought Cambridge Airport and its adjacent land in June - are keen to build a new stadium on their premises though, with the potential to also build 10,000 new homes and a new railway station too.

As a prestigious university city, Cambridge has a bit of untapped potential when it comes to trying to recruit new fans, and there's also a decent enough catchment area to try and bring new faces to watch the club.

At the Abbey Stadium though, new growth will be limited thanks to the lack of expansion on offer for the facilities, but a brand, spanking new complex not far from the original would certainly go down a treat.

It would of course cost a significant amount of money to come to fruition, with AFC Wimbledon's Plough Lane complex costing £34 million to construct, and that only has a capacity of just over 9,000.

Given they're close to maxing out their capacity already at the Abbey Stadium, there won't be that much growth in the next decade, where they intend to stay at their current home, but in 10 years time, they could be ready to move into a new home, and perhaps Championship ambitions can be realised.
