Now that the second session of oral arguments for the 2026-27 term is about to begin, we figured it would be a good time to check in on SCOTUSblog’s inaugural Supreme Court forecasting contest.

To remind you, in collaboration with Good Judgment Inc., this gives SCOTUS obsessives (and non-obsessives) a way to formally predict how the court will decide the most important cases of the term, and to test that collective judgment against the glory and horror that is AI (or, apparently, Super Intelligence). In July, we will provide the names on SCOTUSblog of the three most accurate forecasters, reward them with swag, and compare their predictions to those of the machines.

To make a forecast for these cases, as well as several others yet to be argued, simply visit https://www.gjopen.com/users/sign_up. You only need a working email address, Google account, or Facebook account to do so. Once you sign up, you will be automatically directed to the SCOTUSblog 2026-27 Supreme Court Forecasting Challenge page, where you will be asked to choose the likelihood that each case will be decided one way or the other (and will be able to update your forecast until the case is decided).

As further significant cases are granted, we will add them to the page.
