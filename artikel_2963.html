As AI becomes more fractured, one name stands out: AMD

📅 03 November 2026 | Sumber: Baca Asli

As Anthropic rushes to takes its shares public, the AI trade is showing cracks, and they are not confined to fringe players.

We recently wrote about CoreWeave, which fell 15.5% on Oct. 14 as yields on its 2032 notes touched 15%, and its default-swap spreads nearly doubled in October. IREN is down more than 20% this year. Last Thursday, the contagion reached chipmakers, which makes sense since much of CRWV and IREN's spending is going there. AMD dropped 8.1% as Meta's financing plans and a supply warning from Lam Research exacerbated data-center fears about the pace of AI spending.

Because when you screen for factors that demonstrably generate outperformance over time, the list narrows quickly. My screen: price above the 200-day moving average; revenue growing at least as fast as the S&P 500 (5%+ per annum); EPS growing at least as fast (7%+); rating upgrades; a PEG below 3; and ROE and ROIC of at least 12%. Of the names reporting in the next two weeks, AMD is effectively the only one that makes the list. Yes, you can (and arguably should, as our staples article described) position your portfolio with slightly less tech exposure than the broad indices provide, but we can't ignore the market's biggest and most transformative sector entirely.

Especially because the growth in this case doesn't just meet my criteria; it blows past them. Consensus for Q3 calls for EPS of $1.94 on revenue of $13.06 billion, versus $1.20 and $9.25 billion a year ago. That's YoY growth of nearly 62% and 41%, respectively. The recent drawdown came from a sector selloff, not anything AMD reported, but it shows that if some names in the space get hit, even the larger players are likely to feel it. On Oct. 6, Lisa Su said AMD would substantially increase supply in 2027 because AI orders keep outrunning capacity.

So how do you balance factors we know work for a fast-growing company against a backdrop of cracks in the AI trade? Options.

Owning the stock outright means absorbing a roughly 9% to 11% implied earnings-related move (day of). Earnings are about a week out, and over the past four quarters the smallest move over a similar window was more than 9%, and the largest move, after the company reported its Q1 26 results, was 38%! Of course, that kind of volatility means options aren't cheap, and that rich premium is also a reason not to simply buy calls.

A call spread risk reversal does both jobs: it reduces risk if the cracks widen, and the short options offset the elevated volatility we would otherwise pay for. The bottom chart shows that, by sacrificing about $1,700 of upside for $5,000 in outperformance during a severe downside move, and given how jittery this trade has been lately, this is a way to stay in it with considerably less risk. $5,000 less, specifically. Of course, selling that out will tie up some cash, but less so than simply buying the stock. And selling both the out and the call reduces long call purchase by 66%
